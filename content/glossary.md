---
title: Glossary
---

## Break even
Getting back to the money you started with, so your total result is zero: no gain and no loss.

## Cross margin
A setting where your whole account balance backs a leveraged trade. If the trade goes badly, it can use up everything, not just the margin you put in.

## Drawdown
How far your account (or a price) has fallen from its last high point. A 20% drawdown means you're 20% below your peak.

## Expectancy
What a strategy makes or loses on average per trade over many trades. It combines how often you win with how much you win or lose each time.

## Hypothetical example
A made-up example used to show how something works. The numbers are invented, but the maths is real.

## Index
A basket of investments tracked as one number, such as the S&P 500. It shows how a group of markets or companies is doing overall.

## Isolated margin
A setting where only the margin you chose for a trade can be lost on it. The rest of your account is kept separate.

## Leverage
Borrowing to trade a bigger amount than you have. It makes both gains and losses bigger, and can wipe out an account quickly.

## Liquidation
When an exchange closes your leveraged trade for you because your losses have nearly used up your margin. The money you put down is lost.

## Liquidation price
The price at which the exchange will close your leveraged trade. The higher your leverage, the closer it sits to the price you bought at.

## Liquidity
How easily you can buy or sell something without moving its price much. Low liquidity means wider gaps between buy and sell prices.

## Margin
The money you put down to open a leveraged trade, like a deposit. Leverage lets the trade be bigger than this amount.

## Position size
How much money you put into one trade. Choosing it carefully is one of the main ways to control risk.

## Risk per trade
The amount you're willing to lose on one trade if it goes wrong, often set as a small percentage of your account.

## S&P 500
An index of about 500 large companies listed in the United States. It's often used as a quick read on the US stock market.

## Stop loss
An order you set in advance to sell if the price falls to a certain level, so a loss can't grow beyond what you planned.

## Volatility
How much and how fast a price moves up and down. High volatility means bigger swings in both directions.

## Win rate
The share of your trades that make money. A high win rate alone doesn't mean a strategy is profitable.
