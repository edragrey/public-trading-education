# What trading actually is, step by step

One idea per screen, with a few quick questions. About **4 minutes**.

<!-- step -->

## An exchange of value

At its simplest, trading means exchanging something because you think its value will change.

You might buy and later sell a stock, coin or commodity. Or you might use a contract that gains or loses as its price moves.

<!-- step -->

## Nobody knows the next price

The future price is uncertain. A sensible idea can be wrong. A correct idea can also take a path you cannot tolerate.

That is why a trade needs more than a forecast.

<!-- step -->

## The six questions

Every trade contains six decisions:

1. What am I trading?
2. Why this one?
3. What would make me enter?
4. How much can I lose?
5. Where is the idea wrong?
6. What will I do next?

## Trading is a plan for uncertainty

You do not need perfect answers. You need answers clear enough to write down and review later.

A plan cannot guarantee a win. It can stop you from inventing a new plan every time the price moves.

<!-- step -->

## Trading and investing

Investing often starts with a longer-term belief about an asset, a business or an economy.

Trading usually starts with a defined setup, a time horizon and a plan for what happens if the setup fails. The boundary is not perfectly sharp; the useful question is what your plan is and when you will review it.

<!-- step -->

## Practise before risking money

You can practise the structure of a trade without risking money. Write down an entry, an invalidation point and a reason, then follow what happened.

That is a paper trade. It gives you something to review before money and emotion make the lesson more expensive.

**Key idea:** A trade is a plan for uncertainty, not a promise that your forecast will come true.
