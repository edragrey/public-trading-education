# A correct forecast that still loses

One small idea per screen, with a calculator at the end. About **4 minutes**.

> **Hypothetical example.** The stock is made up, but the arithmetic is exact.

<!-- step -->

## The story

You buy a stock at **$100** because you think it will reach **$150**.

First it falls to **$65**. You get nervous and sell: a **35% loss**.

Then it recovers and climbs to exactly **$150**.

## Right forecast, losing trade

Your **forecast** was right: the price reached $150.

Your **trade** lost 35%, because you sold at the bottom.

<!-- step -->

## Quick check

You lose 35% and have $65 left. To get back to $100, what must the $65 do?

<button type="button" data-opt="c">Grow 100%</button>

<!-- step -->

## Losses and gains aren't symmetrical

**gain needed = loss ÷ (1 − loss)**

| If you lose | You need to gain |
| --- | --- |
| 10% | about 11% |
| 25% | about 33% |
| 35% | about 54% |
| 50% | 100% |

## Key idea

A good forecast doesn't protect you on its own. **Timing, the size of your losses and your emotions** matter just as much. Being right isn't the same as making money. Survival comes first.
