# R-multiples, step by step

One small idea per screen, with a few quick questions. About **3 minutes**.

<!-- step -->

## R is what you risked

**1R = the amount you risked on that trade.** Results in R let you compare a $20 trade with a $500 trade.

> **Hypothetical.** Risk $20, make $40: **+2R**. Risk $20, lose $20: **−1R**.

<!-- step -->

## Quick check

You risk $40 and make $100. How many R?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="a">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">+2.5R</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">+2R</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">+0.4R</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>**+2.5R.** $100 ÷ $40 = 2.5.</p>
</div>

<!-- step -->

## Averages can hide a big loss

Four wins of +$10 and one loss of −$100 is an 80% win rate but **−$12 per trade** on average.

Look at the average, and at the biggest loss.

<!-- step -->

## Quick check

You risk $25 and lose $50 because you moved your stop. What was that in R?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="b">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">−1R</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">−2R</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">−0.5R</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>**−2R.** You lost twice what you planned. Moving stops is dangerous.</p>
</div>

**Key idea:** R measures every trade against what you risked, so different trades can be compared fairly.
