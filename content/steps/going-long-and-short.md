# Going long and short, step by step

One small idea per screen, with a few quick questions. About **4 minutes**.

<!-- step -->

## Long: buy first

**Going long** means buying. You gain if the price **rises**.

> **Hypothetical.** Buy 10 coins at $100, sell at $110. Gain: **$100**.

<!-- step -->

## The limit of a long loss

A plain long can't lose more than you paid. A coin can't go below $0.

<!-- step -->

## Short: sell first

**Going short** means selling something you don't own yet (borrowed), then buying it back later. You gain if the price **falls**.

> **Hypothetical.** Sell 10 coins at $100, buy them back at $90. Gain: **$100**.

## When a short goes wrong

Same trade, but the price rises to $110 and you buy back. Loss: **$100**.

If it rose to $200, the loss would be **$1,000**.

<!-- step -->

## Quick check

Why can a short lose more than you put in?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="b">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">Exchanges charge a penalty</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">A price can rise without a limit</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden><strong>No ceiling.</strong> A long can fall to zero. A short can keep losing as the price climbs.</p>
</div>

<!-- step -->

## Other costs of a short

- Borrowing or funding fees.
- **Squeezes**: shorts forced to buy back push the price up.
- Always use a stop.

<!-- step -->

## You've got it

**Long gains when price rises, short when it falls. A short has no ceiling on its loss, so it needs a stop and extra care.**
