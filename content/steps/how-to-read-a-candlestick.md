# Candlesticks, step by step

One small idea per screen, with picture questions. About **3 minutes**.

> **Hypothetical prices.** The candles are made up to show how reading works.

<!-- step -->

## One candle, one period

Each candle on a chart shows what happened in **one block of time**: an hour, a day, a week.

It packs **four prices** into one shape: **open, high, low, close**.

<!-- step -->

## The body

The thick part is the **body**. It runs from the **open** (first price) to the **close** (last price).

## The wicks

The thin lines are the **wicks**. They reach up to the **high** and down to the **low**: the furthest the price went in that period.

## Green and red

**Green:** closed **higher** than it opened. Up.

**Red:** closed **lower** than it opened. Down. The open is now at the **top** of the body.

## Reading the wicks

A **long wick** means the price went far in one direction, then came back before the close. That direction was tried, and pushed back.

## The honest part

A candle **describes the past**. It doesn't predict the next one.

You'll see named "patterns" everywhere. They're handy words for describing what happened, but none of them reliably tells you what comes next.

<!-- step -->

## You've got it

**Each candle shows four prices: open, high, low and close. It describes what happened, not what comes next.**

Next time you open a chart, pick one candle and read it out loud before you look at the numbers.
