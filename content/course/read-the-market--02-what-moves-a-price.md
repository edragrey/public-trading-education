Last lesson showed the mechanics: a price moves when one side uses up the orders waiting on the other side. This lesson asks the next question: **why** does one side suddenly become more eager?

## The short answer

More people wanting to buy than sell, at the current price, pushes it up. More wanting to sell than buy pushes it down. Everything else on this page is just a reason that changes that balance.

## The main forces

**1. News and new information.** A company reports earnings, a central bank changes interest rates, an exchange gets hacked. New information makes people change their minds about what something is worth.

**2. Money flowing in or out.** When lots of new money arrives (for example, big funds starting to buy), prices tend to rise. When money leaves, they tend to fall. Interest rates matter here: when safe savings pay more, some money leaves riskier things.

**3. Supply.** In crypto especially, new coins being released (unlocks, rewards) adds sellers. Companies issuing new shares does the same for stocks. More supply needs more buyers just to hold the price steady.

**4. Liquidity.** How much is waiting in the order book. In a thin market a small order can move the price a lot. In a deep one it barely moves.

**5. Forced buying and selling.** Remember liquidations? When many leveraged traders get closed out together, the exchange sells (or buys) for them, which pushes the price further and can trigger the next wave.

**6. Sentiment.** How people feel. Fear makes people sell quickly; excitement makes them buy quickly. Stories and hype (lesson 8) work through this.

## A hypothetical day

> **Hypothetical example.** A made-up coin on a made-up day.

1. Morning: quiet. Few orders, small moves.
2. News: a big exchange says it will list the coin. Lots of buyers arrive. Price jumps 15%.
3. Afternoon: early buyers take profits. More sellers. Price gives back half.
4. Evening: a sharp dip triggers a wave of liquidations on leveraged longs. Price drops a further 8% in minutes, then steadies.

Four different forces in one day. Only one of them was "news".

## The honest part: nobody sees all of it

You will never know every reason a price moved. Big funds don't announce their orders. Forced selling isn't on the news. Often, explanations in headlines are written **after** the move, to fit it.

That's why this course never tells you where a price will go. What you **can** do is understand the forces, notice which ones are active, and size your trades (Survive first) so that being wrong is affordable.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. A price rises sharply with no news at all. Is that possible?</summary><p>Yes. A large buyer, a thin order book, or forced buying from leveraged traders can all move prices without any headline.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. Why might lots of new coins being released push a price down?</summary><p>More supply means more potential sellers. It takes more buyers just to keep the price where it is.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. You read a headline explaining exactly why the market fell. Should you trust it completely?</summary><p>Treat it as one possible reason. Many explanations are written after the move, and some forces, like big orders or liquidations, rarely make the news.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Next time you see a big daily move in any market, write down which of the six forces you think played a part. Then look for at least two sources explaining it. Do they agree?

**Key idea:** Prices move when buyers or sellers become more eager. You can learn the forces, but you'll never see all of them, so plan for being wrong.
