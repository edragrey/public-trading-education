**Taking responsibility** in trading means accepting that your results come from your decisions, not from the market, other traders or bad luck. It's a freeing idea, because decisions are the part you can change.

## What it sounds like

- Not: "The market stopped me out and then reversed."
- Instead: "My stop was where my plan said. The result was within my accepted risk."

- Not: "I got scared."
- Instead: "I felt fear, and I didn't follow my rule. What was I telling myself?"

It isn't self-blame. It's looking at your own side of the table, because that's the only side you can improve.

## Practise in a safe way

You learn these habits the way you learn a sport: by repeating small, low-stakes reps.

1. **Paper trade** your written plan until following it feels ordinary.
2. **Go small** with real money, so mistakes are cheap.
3. **Log your state** before each trade: calm, rushed, tired, angry. Notice what predicts rule breaks.
4. **Review weekly:** which trades followed the plan, and which didn't?
5. **Grow slowly,** only after a stretch of followed rules, not after a lucky run.

## Expect setbacks

You'll break rules sometimes. Treat each as data. What happened, what belief or fear was behind it, what small change would help?

## One thing to remember

You're not trying to remove emotion. You're building habits so that emotion doesn't get the final say.

## Common mistakes

- Blaming the market for plan-breaking trades.
- Growing size after a lucky run.
- Treating every rule break as proof you can't do this.
