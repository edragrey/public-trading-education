Every trade has someone on the other side. Knowing who they might be changes how you think about price moves.

## Two words first

- **Participant:** anyone who buys or sells in a market.
- **Market maker:** a firm or bot that constantly offers to both buy and sell, earning the small gap between the two prices (the spread). It keeps trading smooth.

## The main kinds

| Participant | What they do | Size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Retail traders** | Individuals like you, trading their own savings | Small each, large together |
| **Funds and professionals** | Hedge funds and asset managers trading client or firm money | Large, with teams and data |
| **Market makers** | Quote buy and sell prices all day for a living | Large, fast, mostly automated |
| **Long-term holders** | People and companies who buy and hold for years | Vary |
| **Large holders** | In crypto, people holding very large amounts, often called "whales" | Large |

## Why it matters

- **Size moves price.** A large order eats through the order book and pushes price. You, trading small, rarely do.
- **Speed matters.** Automated traders react in milliseconds. You can't win a race against them, so don't race. Use a plan and bigger timeframes.
- **They have reasons you can't see.** A fund may be selling because a client withdrew money, not because it has a view. A price move can have a cause that has nothing to do with the chart.
- **Your edge is not speed or size.** It is patience, small size and a plan.

> **Hypothetical example.** A large holder sells a big block of a small coin over one hour. Price drops 8% on heavy volume. A beginner thinks "the news must be bad". In fact it was one seller, and price recovers the next day. Not every move is information.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming every big move means someone knows something.
- Trying to beat professionals at speed.
- Following "whale" alerts as trade signals.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. What does a market maker earn?</summary><p>The small gap (spread) between its buy and sell prices.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. Why can a small trader rarely move the price?</summary><p>Their orders are tiny compared with the order book.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. Is a big price drop always caused by bad news?</summary><p>No. A single large seller, or a client withdrawal at a fund, can do it.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. Should you try to beat automated traders on speed?</summary><p>No. Use a plan, small size and larger timeframes instead.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. What is a small trader's real edge?</summary><p>Patience, small size, and sticking to a plan.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Pick a coin or stock. Find one day with a big move. Look for a reason (news, a release, a large holder). Write down whether you found one, and what that tells you about guessing the cause.

**Key idea:** The other side of your trade can be a fund, a bot or a large holder with reasons you can't see. Don't read a story into every move.
