A market can only go up if more money comes in than goes out. So traders try to follow the **flow of money**. This lesson shows what that means and how little certainty it gives.

## Two words first

**Inflow** is money (or coins) entering a market, fund or exchange. **Outflow** is money leaving.

**Net flow** is inflows minus outflows over a period.

## Where money enters crypto

- **From banks and cards:** people buying coins with ordinary money.
- **From stablecoins:** dollar-pegged tokens already inside crypto, waiting to be used to buy other coins.
- **From funds:** exchange-traded funds (ETFs) that hold coins on behalf of investors.
- **From new coins** being created and sold.

## What traders watch

- **Stablecoin supply:** a growing amount of stablecoins is often read as "money waiting on the side". It is an interpretation, not a fact: the money may never be spent on other coins.
- **ETF flows:** some funds publish daily figures for money going in or out. These are measured, which makes them more reliable than most flow claims.
- **Exchange flows:** coins moving **to** exchanges may suggest people preparing to sell; moving **off** exchanges may suggest holding. Again, an interpretation.
- **Sector flows:** money moving between corners of the market, such as from big coins to small ones. Hard to measure well.

> **Hypothetical example.** Stablecoin supply has grown for three months and a fund reports its biggest weekly inflow of the year. Many commentators say "money is coming in". But if the price has already risen 40%, those buyers may be late. Flow data tells you what happened, not what happens next.

## What to be careful about

- **Measured vs guessed.** ETF flows are reported. "Whales are buying" is often a guess from wallet data.
- **Lagging.** Flow data usually arrives after price has moved.
- **Double counting.** The same money moving between wallets is not new money.
- **Stories sell.** "Smart money is accumulating" is a popular headline. Ask what was actually measured.

## Money also leaves

When prices fall, money leaves crypto for banks and stablecoins. Liquidity drying up is part of why bear markets feel quiet and thin.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a flow number as a prediction.
- Believing a source that does not say how it measured.
- Buying after a big inflow headline, when the price has already moved.
