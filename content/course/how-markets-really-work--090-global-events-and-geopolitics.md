Markets today are global. A decision in Beijing, a war in Eastern Europe or a vote in a faraway capital can change prices on your screen within minutes. You do not need to be a political expert, but you do need to know how events reach markets.

## Two words first

**Geopolitics** is how power, borders and conflicts between countries affect the world, including the economy.

A **shock** is a sudden, surprising event, such as a war starting or a bank failing. Shocks move markets because nobody had it priced in.

## How events reach prices

- **Risk-off:** uncertainty makes many investors sell risky assets and hold safer ones (the last lesson). Crypto and stocks often fall together on the first day of a shock.
- **Commodities:** conflicts and sanctions can disrupt oil, gas or grain, pushing those prices up, and through them inflation.
- **Inflation and rates:** higher energy and food prices raise inflation, which can change what central banks do.
- **Currencies:** money can flee a country in trouble toward the dollar or other safer currencies.
- **Trade:** tariffs and export limits change company profits and supply chains.
- **Money flows:** sanctions and capital controls can push people toward alternatives, including crypto. That is a story, not a rule.
- **Regulation:** governments announcing rules on crypto can move prices up or down.

> **Hypothetical example.** A conflict escalates overnight. Stocks and crypto drop sharply, oil jumps, and the dollar strengthens. Two weeks later, with no further escalation, prices have partly recovered. The first reaction was fear; the later one was a reassessment.

## Why the first reaction is often wrong

Early moves are driven by fear, thin liquidity and forced selling (the liquidity and leverage lessons). Prices often **overshoot** and then settle once the real effect is clearer. Sometimes the effect is bigger than first thought, sometimes smaller. Nobody knows in the first hour.

## Different events, different clocks

- **Scheduled events** (elections, summits, central bank meetings) can be planned for.
- **Slow-burning events** (trade disputes, long wars) play out over months, and markets partly adapt.
- **Shocks** arrive with no warning and give you no time.

## What a trader can do

- **Keep a calendar** of scheduled risks.
- **Size down** or step aside around events you cannot judge. Stops can slip in shocks (the liquidity lesson).
- **Avoid trading headlines in the first minutes.** Spreads widen and prices whip.
- **Check what is actually confirmed.** Early reports are often wrong or exaggerated.
- **Diversify your thinking:** a global shock can hit all your positions at once.
- **Do not bet on one outcome.** "If X happens, then crypto does Y" has failed many times.

## Common mistakes

- Trading the first headline without confirmation.
- Assuming crypto always rises as a "safe haven" in a crisis.
- Building a big position on a political prediction.
- Ignoring slow-moving stories until they are a shock.
