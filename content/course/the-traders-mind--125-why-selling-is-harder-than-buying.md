Most beginners put a lot of effort into when to buy and almost none into when to sell. Yet selling is where much of the money is made or lost, and it is harder than it looks.

## Two words first

**Regret** is the pain of thinking "I should have done something else". Traders feel it in two forms: sold too early, or held too long.

**Greed** here just means wanting more of a good thing, so you do not want to sell while it is still rising.

## Why buying feels easy and selling does not

Buying is about hope. You imagine what could happen. Selling is about a **result**: you have to decide that this is enough, or that you were wrong. That is harder.

- **When you are up,** selling feels like leaving money on the table, so you wait. Then it drops.
- **When you are down,** selling makes the loss real, so you wait. Then it drops more.

Both come from the same thing: you do not want to feel the regret of being wrong.

## The two regrets

- **"I sold and it kept going."** You feel foolish and start to chase.
- **"I held and it fell back."** You feel foolish and start to sell everything too fast next time.

Whatever you do, price may go the other way. A good exit is not one that guessed the top. It is one that followed your **plan**.

## Habits that help

- **Decide the exit before you buy.** See the lesson on a plan for taking profits.
- **Sell in pieces** if all-or-nothing is too hard. Part sold means some regret either way is smaller.
- **Judge the decision, not the outcome.** Ask "did I follow my plan?" rather than "did price go up after?".
- **Do not watch every tick** while in a trade. More screen time means more urges.
- **Write what you felt** in your journal at the moment you sold or held.

## Common mistakes

- Changing the plan in the middle of the emotion.
- Holding a loser because selling "makes it real".
- Selling winners too early because gains feel fragile.
- Regretting yourself into your next bad trade.
