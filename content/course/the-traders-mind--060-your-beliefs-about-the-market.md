A **belief** is something you treat as true, usually without checking. We all hold beliefs about money, risk and being right. They filter what we see.

## How beliefs shape what you notice

> **Hypothetical example.** Two traders see the same dip. One believes "dips are chances to buy". The other believes "dips mean danger". The first sees opportunity; the second sees warning signs. Same chart, different story.

Neither belief is a fact. They're habits of thought.

## Beliefs that cause trouble

- "I must be right to be a good trader."
- "Losses mean I failed."
- "There's a perfect indicator I haven't found yet."
- "If I just watch closely enough, I can tell what's next."
- "Money I lose is lost for good, so I must win it back."

Each one pushes behaviour you'd regret: hold losers, hunt certainty, chase.

## Beliefs that help

- "Losses are a normal business cost."
- "My job is to follow the plan, not predict."
- "I can be wrong and still be a good trader."
- "No single trade matters much."

## How to change a belief

1. **Notice it.** Write down the thought behind a mistake.
2. **Question it.** Is it a fact, or a habit?
3. **Replace it** with a more useful statement.
4. **Act on the new one**, in small size, repeatedly. Beliefs change through experience more than through reading.

It takes time. Expect progress to be slow and uneven, and don't judge yourself harshly for it.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a belief as a fact.
- Trying to change beliefs by reading alone.
- Being harsh with yourself for old habits of thought.
