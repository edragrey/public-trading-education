The most freeing idea in trading: **a single result tells you very little about whether your decision was good.**

## Luck is part of every trade

Even a good plan loses often. Even a poor plan sometimes wins. Over one trade or ten, luck can swamp skill.

> **Hypothetical example.** A trader breaks their rules, buys on a rumour with no stop, and it jumps 30%. Another follows the plan perfectly and gets stopped out for a small loss. The first trader "won" and the second "lost". Which one should feel good, and which one should worry?

The second made a good decision and took a normal loss. The first had a bad process and got lucky. If the first repeats it, luck eventually runs out.

## The four boxes

| | Good result | Bad result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Good process** | Deserved win | Bad luck: carry on |
| **Bad process** | Dumb luck: dangerous | Deserved loss: fix it |

Most traders feel the top-left and bottom-right boxes. The two others are where the real lessons are.

## What counts as good process

- The trade matched a **written setup**.
- Risk was **inside your limit** and the stop was set.
- Costs were **known**.
- You were **calm and rested**, not chasing.
- You **followed** your exit rules.
- You **logged** it.

If you can tick those, it was a good trade, whatever the result.

## How to grade yourself

In your journal, give every trade two separate marks:

1. **Process score** (for example 0 to 5, from the list above).
2. **Result** (in R).

In your weekly review, look at the process scores first. Results come second. Aim to raise the average process score. Over time, results tend to follow, but you control the process and not the result.

## Why this helps your mind

- You stop swinging between elation and despair on every trade.
- You can take a loss without doubting everything.
- You can refuse a lucky win as a reason to bend the rules.
- You can keep trading through a normal bad streak.

## What it isn't

Process doesn't excuse a system that never works. If your sets of trades, followed perfectly, lose money over a **large** sample, the system needs to change (see the when to change your rules lesson). Process over outcome applies to single trades and short runs, not to ignoring the data forever.

## Common mistakes

- Praising yourself for lucky wins.
- Blaming yourself for normal losses.
- Changing the system after one loss.
- Having no process score, so there's nothing to improve.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. A trader breaks rules and wins. Is that a good trade?</summary><p>No. The result was good, the process wasn't.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. A trader follows the plan and takes a small loss. Good or bad trade?</summary><p>A good trade. The process was right and the loss is normal.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. What do you score each trade on?</summary><p>A process score and a result.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. What is "resulting"?</summary><p>Judging a decision by how it turned out instead of by how it was made.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. When should the system itself change?</summary><p>After a large sample of followed trades shows it doesn't work, not after one loss.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Look at your last five trades (real or paper). Give each a process score from 0 to 5 using the checklist, and then note the result. Which box of the four did each fall in?

**Key idea:** Grade the decision, not the outcome. Good process wins over time; luck evens out.
