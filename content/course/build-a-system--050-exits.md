Beginners plan the entry and hope for the exit. A system plans **both exits first**.

## The two exits

1. **The loss exit (stop):** where the idea is wrong. This protects you. See the stops lesson.
2. **The win exit:** how and where you take profit.

## Ways to take profit

- **Fixed target:** a set price, often chosen from a recent high or a multiple of your risk (for example 2R, twice the amount you risked). Clear and simple. You may leave gains on the table if the move runs.
- **Trailing stop:** the stop follows price up as it moves in your favour. You can stay in a long trend, but you give back some gain when it turns.
- **Partial exits:** sell part at a target and let the rest run. It trades some upside for calmer nerves.
- **Time exit:** leave after a set number of days if nothing has happened. It stops money sitting in dead trades.
- **Signal exit:** leave when a condition fails (for example, price closes below a moving average).

> **Hypothetical example.** You buy at $50 with a stop at $48 (risk $2, so 1R = $2). A fixed target at 2R is $54. Alternatively, once price reaches $52 you move the stop to $50 (your entry), so the trade can no longer lose, and let it run with a trailing stop.

## The trade-offs

No exit is best. Each costs something:

- Tight stop: more small losses.
- Wide stop: bigger losses, but it needs a smaller position.
- Early targets: a higher win rate, but each win is small.
- Letting winners run: fewer wins, bigger ones.

The win rate and expectancy lesson shows that what matters is the **combination** of how often you win and how much you win or lose.

## Never move a stop further away

You can move a stop to **reduce** risk (for example up to break-even). Moving it further out because you don't want to take the loss turns a planned loss into an open-ended one.

## Common mistakes

- Setting targets from hope instead of structure.
- Taking small profits early and holding losers.
- Moving the stop wider "just this once".
- Having no exit for a winning trade, then giving it all back.

## Try it (no real money)

Add two exit lines to your trade plan: your stop rule and your profit rule. Test them on three past setups on a chart and write down what each would have done.

**Key idea:** Plan both exits before you enter. Every exit style has a cost, so pick one, write it down and keep it.
