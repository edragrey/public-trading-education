You already know an uptrend makes higher lows. A **trendline** simply joins those lows with a line, so you can see the slope of the trend at a glance.

## Two words first

A **trendline** is a straight line drawn through the swing lows of an uptrend (or the swing highs of a downtrend).

A **channel** is a second line, drawn parallel to the trendline on the other side of the price, so the price seems to move between two rails.

## How to draw one

1. Find two clear **swing lows** in an uptrend (the market structure lesson shows how).
2. Draw a line through them and extend it to the right.
3. A **third touch** that bounces makes the line more believable. Two points make a line; three make it a pattern.
4. For a channel, copy the line and drag it to the highest swing high in between.

> **Hypothetical example.** A coin makes lows at $40 and $44 a few weeks apart, then dips to the line again around $48 and bounces. The line has now been touched three times. A parallel line drawn through the highs, near $58, completes a channel. The price is rising inside it.

## What a trendline tells you

- **The slope:** a steep line is fast and often fragile. A gentle one is more sustainable.
- **Where a dip might stop:** buyers have stepped in near the line before.
- **When the trend may be changing:** a clear break below the line, especially followed by a lower high, is a warning.

## What a break does and does not mean

A break is a sign, not a verdict. Price can poke through a line and snap back. Traders often wait for a **close** beyond the line, and for a move that is not just one candle.

## Rules of thumb

- Use clear swing points, not tiny wiggles.
- Don't force a line through points that don't fit. If you have to bend it, there is no trendline.
- Lines drawn on a **higher timeframe** (daily, weekly) matter more than ones on a 5-minute chart.
- Lines get redrawn as the trend steepens or flattens. That is normal.

## Common mistakes

- Drawing a line to fit the story you want.
- Treating a touch as a guaranteed bounce.
- Placing a stop just beyond a line everyone can see.
- Ignoring the timeframe the line was drawn on.

## Try it (no real money)

Open a daily chart in an uptrend. Draw a line through two swing lows, extend it, and see how many later touches it gets.

**Key idea:** a trendline joins swing points to show the slope of a trend. It is a guide that can break, not a promise.
