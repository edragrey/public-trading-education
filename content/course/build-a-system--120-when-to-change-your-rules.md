Sooner or later your system will have a rough patch, and you'll want to change it. Sometimes that's right. Often it's the worst moment to do it. This lesson gives you a simple way to decide.

## One word first

**Variance** is the normal up-and-down of results that comes from chance. Even a good system has losing weeks and losing months, just from variance.

## The two most common mistakes

**Changing too fast.** Three losses in a row, so you add a new rule. Two more losses, so you change the stop method. After a month you've tested five systems for a week each, and learned nothing about any of them.

**Never changing.** Ignoring clear evidence over many trades because "the system is the system". Rules should evolve; they just shouldn't evolve every Tuesday.

## Before changing anything, ask three questions

**1. Did I actually follow the rules?**
Check your journal (the journal lesson). If the losses come from broken rules, the problem is **behaviour**, not the system. Changing the rules won't help.

**2. Is the sample big enough?**
Under 30 trades, results are mostly variance (see the testing lesson). Wait.

**3. Is the problem specific?**
"It isn't working" isn't specific. "Losses are bigger than −1R on a third of trades, all of them in the first hour after the US market opens" is. A specific problem suggests a specific fix.

## How to change, when you do

- **One change at a time.** Otherwise you'll never know which change helped.
- **Write it down** with the date, as a new plan version (see the trade plan lesson).
- **Give it enough trades** (30 or more) before judging it.
- **Compare versions** using your journal, in R.

## Good reasons and bad reasons

| Usually a good reason | Usually a bad reason |
| --- | --- |
| After 40+ trades following the plan, losses keep exceeding −1R | Three losses in a row |
| A rule keeps being broken because it's unclear | A friend's system did better this week |
| The market you trade has clearly changed (much lower volume, new hours) | You saw a new indicator on social media |
| A specific condition keeps showing up in losing trades | You're bored with the rules |

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. You've lost 5 trades in a row. Should you change your system?</summary><p>Not yet. First check if you followed the rules, and whether you have enough trades to judge. Five losses in a row is normal variance.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. Why change only one thing at a time?</summary><p>So you can tell which change made the difference.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. Your losses mostly come from trades where you broke a rule. Should you change the rules?</summary><p>No. The problem is following them. Work on that first.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Write your own "change policy" at the bottom of your trade plan. For example: "I only change a rule after 30 trades, one change at a time, and only for a specific problem I can point to in my journal."

**Key idea:** Check your behaviour first, wait for enough trades, then change one specific thing at a time.
