Every trade has two sides. When you buy, someone sells. It helps to know who that might be, because they are often better informed, better funded or simply playing a different game.

## The main players

- **Retail traders:** individuals like you, usually with small accounts and often reacting to news and social media.
- **Whales:** holders of very large amounts. A single whale order can move a small market.
- **Market makers:** firms that continuously post both buy and sell prices, earning the small gap (the spread) between them. They are paid to provide liquidity, not to bet on direction.
- **Funds and institutions:** professional money with research, rules and size. They tend to trade in pieces so as not to move the price.
- **Exchanges:** the venues that match orders and earn fees. Some also run their own trading.
- **Miners, validators and developers:** people who receive coins for their work and sometimes sell them.

## What they want

> **Hypothetical example.** A market maker posts a buy at $99.95 and a sell at $100.05. If someone sells to them and another buys from them, they earn $0.10 per coin without caring where the price goes next.

A market maker wants the **spread** and a quick turnover. A fund may want to build a position quietly over days. A whale may want to sell without crashing the price, so spreads the sale out. A retail trader often wants a quick gain.

Different goals mean different behaviour, which is part of why prices move the way they do.

## What this means for you

- Someone usually has **more information, speed or size** than you. You do not need to beat them. You need to avoid trading when you are at a disadvantage.
- Be careful in thin markets: you may be selling to someone who knows more than you.
- Large wallets are visible on some blockchains. That is a clue, but not a signal: the owner might be moving funds between their own accounts.
- A coin with only a few big holders can be **pushed around** by them.

## Why prices can look "manipulated"

In small coins, large players can move the price with relatively little money. Be especially wary of sudden pumps in thin markets, and of anyone promoting a coin they hold. This is a **safety fact**, not an accusation: it is just the way small, thin markets work.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming everyone else is as small and slow as you.
- Following a whale's wallet without knowing why they moved.
- Trading thin coins that a few holders dominate.

<details><summary>2. Why do funds trade in pieces?</summary><p>To avoid moving the price against themselves.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. What is a whale?</summary><p>A holder of a very large amount, big enough to move a small market.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. Why be careful in thin markets?</summary><p>A few large players can move the price, and the person on the other side may know more.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Pick a coin and look up its top holders on a block explorer. Note how much the top 10 hold and whether that worries you.

**Key idea:** every trade has another side with its own goals, so avoid fights where you are at a disadvantage.
