Being up on a trade feels great, and that is the moment judgment gets worst. A written profit plan decides **in advance**, when you are calm, what you will do when you are happy.

## Two words first

**Taking profit** means selling some or all of a winning position so the gain becomes real money instead of a number on a screen.

**Scaling out** means selling a position in pieces instead of all at once.

## Why the plan comes first

Without a plan, one of two things usually happens. You sell too early because any gain feels fragile, or you hold on hoping for more, and watch a good gain shrink to nothing. Both come from deciding in the middle of the emotion.

## The three questions

Write down answers before you enter:

1. **Where is my first target?** Tie it to the chart (a recent high, a level where price stalled) or to a multiple of your risk (for example 2R).
2. **How much will I sell there?** All, half, a third.
3. **What happens to the rest?** A trailing stop, a later target, or a time limit.

## One example, not advice

> **Hypothetical example.** You buy at $100 with a stop at $95, so 1R = $5. Your plan:
> - At $110 (2R): sell **half**. Move the stop on the rest to your entry, $100.
> - Then use a trailing stop on the other half.
>
> If price hits $110 and then falls, you banked half your gain and the rest costs you nothing. If it keeps running, you still own half of it. If the stop at $95 is hit first, you lose 1R as planned.

This is only one way. Many traders sell all at the first target, others use thirds. The right plan is the one you can **follow without arguing with yourself**, and that you have tested on paper first.

## What a good plan does

- Gives you a rule for the happy moment, not just the scary one.
- Makes wins repeatable, because you do the same thing each time.
- Protects you from both selling too soon and from round-tripping a gain.

## Common mistakes

- Having a plan only for the stop.
- Changing the target upward because price got close and you feel greedy.
- Selling everything at the first small gain, every time, and never letting a winner develop.
- Setting targets from hope, not from the chart.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. Why write the profit plan before entering?</summary><p>Because you decide better when calm. Once you are up, emotion pushes you to sell too early or hold too long.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. Entry $100, stop $95. What price is 2R?</summary><p>Risk is $5, so 2R is $10 above entry: $110.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. What does scaling out mean?</summary><p>Selling a position in pieces instead of all at once.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. After selling half at 2R, where might the stop on the rest go?</summary><p>To your entry price, so the rest cannot turn into a loss.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. Is there one correct profit plan?</summary><p>No. The best plan is one you tested and can follow without arguing with yourself.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Take a paper trade. Before entering, write the three answers: first target, how much to sell, and what happens to the rest.

**Key idea:** decide how you will sell before you buy, so the happy moment is run by a plan and not by emotion.
