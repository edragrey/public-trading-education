Now you can read candles and choose a timeframe, you can start describing what a chart has been doing. Traders use four words for this all the time. This lesson explains them plainly, and is honest about what they can't do.

## Trend

A **trend** is a general direction over time.

- **Uptrend:** the price keeps making higher highs and higher lows. Each dip stops above the last dip.
- **Downtrend:** lower highs and lower lows. Each bounce stops below the last bounce.

A trend is a description of the past. It can continue, pause or reverse, and nobody knows in advance which.

## Range

A **range** is when the price moves sideways, bouncing between a rough ceiling and a rough floor, without going clearly up or down. Many markets spend a lot of their time in ranges.

## Support and resistance

**Support** is a price area where falls have stopped before, because buyers stepped in.

**Resistance** is a price area where rises have stopped before, because sellers stepped in.

> **Hypothetical example.** A made-up coin over three months.

- It fell to around **$48** three times, and each time bounced. Traders would call **$48 an area of support**.
- It rose to around **$60** twice, and each time fell back. They'd call **$60 an area of resistance**.
- Between them, the coin was in a **range** from about $48 to $60.

## Why these areas can matter

There's no magic in a number. These areas matter only because **people remember them**. Buyers who missed the last bounce at $48 may place orders there. Sellers who were stuck at $60 may sell when they get their money back. Lots of people watching the same area can make it act like a floor or ceiling, for a while.

## Areas, not lines

Support and resistance are **zones**, not exact prices. A price might bounce at $48.30 one time and $47.60 the next. Drawing a thin line and expecting the price to respect it to the cent leads to frustration.

## They break

Every support eventually breaks and every resistance eventually gives way. When support breaks, it sometimes becomes resistance later (and the other way round), but not always. **Nothing on this page tells you what the price will do next.**

## How this helps you, honestly

The real use of these ideas for a beginner is **planning**, not predicting:

- **Placing stops.** Survive first said: put your stop where your idea is proven wrong. If your idea is "buyers keep stepping in around $48", then a clear break below that zone is where you're wrong.
- **Choosing targets.** A realistic target is often near the last area where the price turned around, not a number you'd like it to reach.
- **Staying out.** If the price is in the middle of a range, with no clear area near your entry, there may be no trade at all. That's fine.

## Try it (no real money)

On a daily chart, find one area where the price turned around at least twice. Mark it as a zone, not a line. Then ask: if I bought near here, where would my idea be proven wrong? That's your stop.

**Key idea:** Trends, ranges, support and resistance describe where a price has been. Use them to plan stops and targets, never as promises about where it's going.
