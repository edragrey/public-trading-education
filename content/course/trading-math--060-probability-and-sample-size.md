You made money on 7 of your last 10 trades. Are you good? Maybe. Maybe you were lucky. This lesson gives you a plain way to tell the difference.

## Two words first

**Probability** is the chance something happens, from 0% (never) to 100% (always). A coin flip is 50%.

**Sample size** is how many trades (or flips) you are judging from.

## Luck looks like skill in small samples

> **Hypothetical example.** Imagine a trader who is truly 50/50: no edge at all. Out of 10 trades, they will win 7 or more about **17% of the time** (roughly 1 in 6) just by luck. Out of 100 trades, winning 70 or more is far rarer, well under 1 in 1,000.

With few trades, almost anything can happen by chance. With many, the results settle toward the truth. That is why one great week proves nothing and one bad week proves nothing.

## Streaks happen in random data

Flip a fair coin 100 times and a run of 6 or 7 heads or tails in a row is **normal**. A losing streak does not mean you are broken, and a winning streak does not mean you are skilled. This is why the losing streak lesson tells you to plan for runs of losses.

## How many trades is enough?

There is no magic number, but as a rough guide:

- **10 trades:** almost nothing.
- **30 trades:** a hint, no more.
- **100 trades:** starting to mean something, if recorded honestly and the market did not change much.
- **200+:** a reasonable picture.

And the results only count if the trades followed the **same rules**. A mix of random trades is not a sample of anything.

## Two traps

- **Gambler's fallacy:** thinking a win is "due" after a string of losses. Each trade is separate. The market does not owe you a win.
- **Hot-hand fallacy:** thinking you are on a roll and can raise the size. Streaks are normal in random data.

## What to do with this

- Judge a system on 50 to 100 trades, not on a week.
- Record every trade, including losers, so the sample is honest.
- Change your rules rarely, and only after enough trades (see the lesson on when to change your rules).
- While the sample is small, trade small.

## Common mistakes

- Dropping a good system after a short bad run.
- Raising size after a short good run.
- Throwing out losing trades from your record.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. A trader wins 7 of 10. Does that prove skill?</summary><p>No. Someone with no edge does this roughly 1 time in 6.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. A fair coin lands heads 6 times in a row. Is the next flip more likely to be tails?</summary><p>No. Each flip is 50/50. That belief is the gambler's fallacy.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. Roughly how many honest trades before a result starts to mean something?</summary><p>Around 100, following the same rules.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. You are on a 5-win streak. Should you double your size?</summary><p>No. Streaks are normal in random data and say little about your edge.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. Why record losing trades too?</summary><p>If you keep only winners, your sample is made-up and your numbers lie to you.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Flip a coin 20 times, noting heads as wins. Write the longest streak you see. Then do it 3 more times and compare.

**Key idea:** small samples are mostly luck, so gather many honest trades before you trust a result.
