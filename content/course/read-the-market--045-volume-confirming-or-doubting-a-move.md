Price tells you **where** a market moved. **Volume** tells you **how many people** took part. A move that lots of people joined is usually more believable than one a few people pushed.

## Two words first

**Volume** is how much was traded in a period: coins, shares or dollars changing hands. Charts show it as bars at the bottom.

**Confirmation** means volume agrees with the price move, which makes the move more believable.

## What volume bars show

Each bar sits under a candle. A tall bar means many trades in that period; a short bar means few. Compare bars to the **recent normal**, not to a fixed number.

## Confirming a move

> **Hypothetical example.** A coin sits in a range for weeks with low, quiet volume. Then it closes above the top of the range on a candle with **three times the usual volume**. Many traders joined the break, so it is more believable than a break on thin volume.

- **Breakout with high volume:** more believable.
- **Trend moving with rising volume:** healthy participation.

## Doubting a move

- **Breakout on thin volume:** few people behind it, so it can fail and fall back.
- **Price makes a new high but volume is lower than on the last high:** fewer people are chasing. It may be tiring.
- **A huge spike in volume at the end of a long run:** sometimes a sign of excitement near a top, but it is only a clue.

## Pullbacks

In a healthy uptrend, dips often have **lower** volume than the rises. Few sellers join the dip. A dip with **big** volume asks a harder question.

## The honest limits

- Volume is a clue, not a signal. A high-volume move can still fail.
- In crypto, volume can be reported differently on different exchanges, and some is fake (the volume and liquidity lesson covers this).
- Volume has daily rhythms: it is usually lower at quiet hours. Compare like with like.

## Common mistakes

- Reading volume without comparing it to recent bars.
- Treating one tall bar as a signal on its own.
- Trusting volume figures from unknown venues.

## Try it (no real money)

On a daily chart, find a breakout above a range. Look at the volume bar under it and compare it with the previous ten.
