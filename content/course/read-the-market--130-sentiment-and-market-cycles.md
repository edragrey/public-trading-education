Prices reflect the facts, but also **how people feel** about them. That mood is called **sentiment**.

## Fear and greed

- **Greed:** people buy because prices are rising and they fear missing out. Prices can stretch beyond what the facts support.
- **Fear:** people sell because prices are falling and they fear losing more. Prices can drop below what the facts support.

Neither is permanent, and nobody can time the turn exactly.

## How you can see sentiment

- **Social feeds:** everyone is posting wins, or everyone is panicking.
- **Search interest:** how often people look up a coin or topic.
- **Funding rates and open interest:** in derivatives, extreme numbers can show crowded one-sided bets.
- **Fear and greed indexes:** some sites combine several signals into one score. Treat them as a mood gauge, not a signal.

## The classic cycle

Markets tend to move through phases, although never on a schedule:

1. **Quiet:** few people care, prices are low.
2. **Rising:** early buyers are rewarded, more notice it.
3. **Excitement:** headlines, big gains, newcomers rush in.
4. **Peak:** the mood is euphoric. Many say "this time is different".
5. **Fall:** prices drop, hope turns to worry, then panic.
6. **Gloom:** people say it's over and stop watching. The cycle begins again.

> **Hypothetical example.** A coin rises for months. A taxi driver, a neighbour and a coworker all ask you how to buy it. A rise in attention from people who never cared before is often *a sign of late excitement*, not proof of a top. Many times the move goes further.

## Crowded trades

When almost everyone is on one side, there are few buyers left to push the price up. That makes sharp reversals more likely. But a "crowded" trade can stay crowded for a long time.

## What to actually do with it

- Be **more careful** when everyone is euphoric: smaller size, tighter plan.
- Be **calm** when everyone is afraid: don't sell at the lows just because the feed is red.
- Use sentiment as context, never as the only reason to trade.
- Stick to your written plan. That's your defence against the crowd, and against your own feelings.

## Common mistakes

- Selling every time a feed turns bearish.
- Calling a top because of one sentiment reading.
- Believing you're immune to the crowd.
