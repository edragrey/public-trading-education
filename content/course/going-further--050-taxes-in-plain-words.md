**This is general education, not tax advice.** Rules differ by country and change often. Check your own country's tax authority or a qualified adviser before you rely on any of this.

## Why trading creates tax questions

In most countries, **profit from selling an investment is taxable**, and sometimes losses can reduce the tax you owe. The details vary:

- **What counts as a taxable event:** usually selling, and in many places also swapping one coin for another, or spending crypto.
- **How long you held it** can change the rate (in some countries short holdings are taxed more than long ones).
- **Which category of income** it falls into (investment gains, ordinary income, business income).
- **Where you must report it**, including assets held abroad.

## Records are the real job

Nobody can work out gains without records. Keep, for every trade:

- date and time
- what you bought or sold
- price in your local currency
- amount and fees
- which platform or wallet

Export your history from each platform regularly. Platforms close, change formats or lose old data. Keep copies yourself. A spreadsheet works, and so does your trading journal.

## Gains, losses and cost basis

Your **cost basis** is what you paid, including fees. Your gain is what you sold for minus the cost basis.

> **Hypothetical example.** You buy 10 coins at $50 ($500 plus $5 fees = $505 cost basis) and sell all at $60 ($600, minus $6 fees = $594). Gain = $594 - $505 = **$89**.

If you bought at different prices at different times, rules about **which coins count as sold first** matter (for example first-in-first-out or others). These differ by country.

## A few examples (check current rules)

**United States.** The IRS treats crypto as property, and selling or swapping it can create a capital gain or loss. In general, gains on assets held a year or less are taxed differently from those held longer. For stocks, there is also a **wash sale rule**: if you sell at a loss and buy the same stock again within a short window, you may not be able to claim the loss right away. Brokers send forms of your sales for stocks; for crypto, you may need to track more yourself.

**Spain.** Gains from selling investments, including crypto, are generally reported in the annual income tax return as capital gains, with losses sometimes offset against gains within limits. Crypto held on platforms outside Spain may also trigger **extra reporting obligations** above certain values. Check the Agencia Tributaria's pages or an adviser for the current thresholds, forms and deadlines.

**Elsewhere.** Many countries have their own versions. Search for your tax authority's guidance on "crypto" and "capital gains".

## Practical habits

1. **Keep records from day one.**
2. **Don't spend money you'll owe in tax.** Some traders set aside a share of each gain.
3. **Learn what counts as a taxable event** before you swap or spend.
4. **Use reputable tax software or an adviser** if you trade often.
5. **Never hide income.** The cost of getting it wrong is higher than the tax.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking an unrealised gain isn't tracked, and a withdrawal to a bank is the only event.
- Losing exchange history after a platform shuts down.
- Ignoring small trades. They still count.
- Waiting until deadline week.
