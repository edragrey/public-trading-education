Most of this course works for any market. But crypto, stocks and commodities each have their own habits, and knowing them helps you plan. This lesson puts them side by side.

## What you're actually buying

**Stocks** (shares) are small pieces of a company. If the company does well over time, its shares tend to be worth more. Some pay a part of their profits to shareholders, called dividends.

**Commodities** are raw materials like gold, oil, wheat or copper. Most people trade them through **futures** (contracts to buy or sell at a set price on a future date) or through funds that track their price, rather than owning barrels of oil.

**Crypto** coins and tokens are digital assets that live on a blockchain. What gives a particular coin value varies a lot from project to project, and for many tokens there's no company, profit or physical thing behind them.

## Opening hours

| Market | When it trades |
| --- | --- |
| Crypto | 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, including holidays |
| US stocks (main session) | 9:30 am to 4:00 pm New York time, Monday to Friday |
| Spanish stocks (main session) | 9:00 am to 5:30 pm Madrid time, Monday to Friday |
| Commodity futures | Nearly round the clock on weekdays for many contracts, with short daily breaks |

Some brokers offer extra hours before and after the main session, usually with less trading and wider spreads.

## Why opening hours matter for your risk

**Markets that close can gap.** If bad news arrives at night or over a weekend, a stock can open far below where it closed. Your stop can't fill in between, so it fills at the opening price, which may be much worse.

**Markets that never close never rest.** Crypto can move hard at 3 am on a Sunday while you're asleep. Stops matter even more, and so does not holding positions you can't afford to have move overnight.

## How wild they swing

Speaking generally, and with lots of exceptions:

- **Large company stocks** and **broad indexes** like the S&P 500 tend to move less day to day.
- **Commodities** can swing a lot around supply news (weather, wars, production decisions).
- **Crypto**, especially smaller coins, often moves much more. Double-digit daily moves aren't unusual for small tokens.

Bigger swings mean wider stops, which means **smaller positions** for the same risk (Survive first, lesson 3).

## Who keeps an eye on them

Stock markets are closely regulated, for example by the SEC in the US and the CNMV in Spain. Companies must publish regular reports. Commodity futures exchanges are regulated too.

Crypto rules are newer and vary by country. The EU has introduced a framework called MiCA for crypto service providers, but protections are still generally thinner than for stocks. That's one more reason the safety checklist in Survive first matters.

## Try it (no real money)

Pick one market of each kind, for example a big stock index, gold, and a well-known coin. For each, look at the daily chart for the last month and note the biggest single-day move. Which swings the most?

**Key idea:** Different markets have different hours, owners and swings. Know which one you're in, and size your risk to match.
