Most beginners think trading is a knowledge problem: learn enough patterns and the money follows. Plenty of people know a lot and still trade badly. The missing piece is usually **how they respond to what happens**.

## Almost no rules

In most jobs, someone else sets the structure. Your hours, your tasks, your boundaries. A market doesn't. Nothing stops you buying more, holding a loser, or jumping in late. The only limits are the ones **you** build.

That freedom is exciting, and it's also the risk. If you don't supply the structure, your mood does.

## The market doesn't hurt you, your reaction does

> **Hypothetical example.** Price drops 3% right after you buy. The market did nothing personal. It moved. The pain comes from what you tell yourself next: "I'm wrong", "I'll lose it all", "I must fix this now". Those thoughts push you to close early, add more, or move your stop.

The move is just information. The reaction is the part you control.

## Two skills, not one

- **Analysis:** finding setups, reading charts.
- **Self-management:** following your plan when it's uncomfortable.

Analysis gets taught everywhere. Self-management gets learned the hard way. This module is about the second one.

## What changes if you accept this

- A losing trade stops being a verdict on you.
- Your job becomes **executing a plan well**, not predicting the future.
- Mistakes become things to measure and fix, not things to hide.

## Common mistakes

- Believing more analysis will fix emotional mistakes.
- Treating every price move as personal.
- Having no written rules and relying on mood.

<details><summary>2. Is trading mainly a knowledge problem?</summary><p>Not only. Knowing a lot and acting badly is common, so self-management matters as much as analysis.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. A price falls 3% right after your entry. What's the market doing, and what's causing your stress?</summary><p>The market is just moving. The stress comes from the story you tell yourself about it.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. Name the two skills this lesson separates.</summary><p>Analysis, and self-management (following the plan under pressure).</p></details>

<details><summary>5. Why write rules before the trade?</summary><p>Because your calm self makes better decisions than your stressed self in the middle of it.</p></details>
