You've entered with a stop and a plan. Now the market moves, and so do your emotions. This lesson is about what to do, and what **not** to do, while a trade is open.

## What you should do

- **Check that the stop is in.** Right after the fill.
- **Check the news calendar.** A big scheduled event can gap past a stop.
- **Follow your written rules** for moving the stop or taking profit.

## What you may change (if your plan says so)

- **Move the stop to reduce risk** (for example to break-even after price reaches a set level).
- **Take partial profit** at a planned level.
- **Exit early if your idea is clearly invalid**, for example the structure you relied on has broken.

## What you shouldn't do

- **Move the stop further away** because the loss feels bad.
- **Add to a losing trade** to "average down" with no plan.
- **Remove the stop** in a fast market.
- **Stare at the screen** and let each candle change your mind.
- **Close a trade only because you're nervous**, when nothing about the idea has changed.

> **Hypothetical example.** You're long with a stop below a swing low. Price drops close to your stop and your stomach tightens. The plan says: leave it. You leave it, the stop is hit for a planned 1R loss, and you move on. The alternative was moving it, taking a 3R loss, and learning nothing useful.

## Telling "nervous" from "invalid"

Ask: **has anything changed about the reasons I entered?**

- If **yes** (for example the structure broke), the plan may say to exit.
- If **no**, only your feelings changed. That's the signal to do nothing.

## Reduce the temptation

- Size so a loss at your stop is genuinely tolerable.
- Set alerts instead of watching.
- Close the chart. Many traders check at fixed times.
- Write a note on how you feel; it helps in the review.

## Common mistakes

- Watching every tick of a swing trade.
- Moving stops and targets in the heat of the moment.
- Adding size to win back a loss.
- Ignoring planned events.

## Go deeper

<details><summary>Why averaging down is risky</summary><p>Adding to a loser means putting more money into a trade that's already proving you wrong. If it keeps falling, the loss grows faster. Planned scaling in, with a clear stop for the whole position, is different from hoping.</p></details>

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. What's the first thing to check after a fill?</summary><p>That your stop is in place.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. Which stop move is acceptable: further away, or to break-even?</summary><p>To break-even (reducing risk). Never further away.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. Price nears your stop and you feel nervous, but nothing about the idea changed. What do you do?</summary><p>Follow the plan and leave it.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. What does "averaging down" mean?</summary><p>Adding to a losing position to lower your average price.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. Name one way to reduce the urge to meddle.</summary><p>Set alerts, check at fixed times, or size so the loss is tolerable.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

On your next paper trade, write down every urge you had to change something, and what you did. Review which urges were about the idea and which were about feelings.

**Key idea:** Once in, follow the plan. You can reduce risk, but never widen a stop, and treat feelings as feelings, not information.
