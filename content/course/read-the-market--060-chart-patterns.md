Traders name shapes that prices often draw. Here are the common ones, and a warning about all of them.

## What a pattern is

A **chart pattern** is a recognisable shape in price, built from swing highs and lows (see the structure lesson). People name it because they've seen similar shapes before, and because many people watch the same ones.

## A few common patterns

- **Double top:** price hits a high, falls, rises to about the same high, and fails. Some read it as buyers failing twice at the same area. A **double bottom** is the mirror image.
- **Triangle:** highs get lower while lows get higher, so price squeezes into a point. It describes pressure building, with no promise about direction.
- **Flag:** a sharp move, then a small, tidy drift against it, before the move may continue.
- **Head and shoulders:** three peaks with the middle one highest. Some read it as an uptrend losing strength.
- **Range breakout:** price leaves a long sideways band.

## The honest part

- **Patterns are clearer afterwards.** On a finished chart every shape looks obvious. Live, many "patterns" never complete.
- **They're subjective.** Two traders can draw different lines on the same chart.
- **Results are mixed.** No pattern works every time. A pattern is a guide to where you'd be wrong, not a forecast.
- **Breakouts often fail.** Price pokes out, pulls people in, and returns. That's a **false breakout**.

> **Hypothetical example.** A coin forms a double top near $80. A trader sells and puts a stop just above $80. If it breaks above, they're out with a small loss. If it falls, they've profited. The pattern gave a clear place to be wrong, and that is its real use.

## A better way to use a pattern

1. See a possible pattern.
2. Ask: **where does this idea fail?** Put the stop there.
3. Size the trade so that failing costs about 1% of your account.
4. Accept that it may simply not work.

## Common mistakes

- Seeing patterns that are not there because you want to trade.
- Entering before the pattern is complete.
- Ignoring the bigger trend and the timeframe.
- Treating a name like "head and shoulders" as a prediction.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. What does a double top describe?</summary><p>Price failing twice at about the same high.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. Does a triangle tell you which way price will break?</summary><p>No. It shows pressure building, not direction.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. What is a false breakout?</summary><p>Price leaves a range, then returns, trapping people who entered.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. Why are patterns easy to see on old charts?</summary><p>You already know how they ended.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. What's the best practical use of a pattern?</summary><p>To define where the idea is wrong, so you can place a stop and size the trade.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Find five "double tops" on any chart. For each, note whether price fell, ranged or broke up afterwards. Count how many worked.

**Key idea:** Patterns are shapes people agree on, easy to see afterwards and uncertain live. Use them to decide where you're wrong, not to predict.
