Markets rarely rise forever or fall forever. They move in **waves**, from optimism to fear and back. Knowing the shape helps you stay calm, but it is not a timetable.

## Two words first

A **bull market** is a long period of generally rising prices. A **bear market** is a long period of generally falling prices.

A **cycle** is one full swing from a low, up through a high, and back down.

## The four phases

1. **Accumulation:** after a long fall, prices are low and quiet. Few are interested. Patient buyers start to build positions.
2. **Markup (bull):** prices climb, news turns good and more people join.
3. **Distribution:** prices stall near highs. Excitement is highest and people who bought early quietly sell to late buyers.
4. **Markdown (bear):** prices fall, hope turns to fear, and many sell at a loss.

> **Hypothetical example.** A coin sits near $10 for months while nobody talks about it (accumulation). Then it rises to $50 as more people notice (markup). It then zigzags between $45 and $55 for weeks while headlines are glowing (distribution), and finally slides to $15 (markdown).

## Why cycles happen

- **Psychology:** fear and greed feed on themselves.
- **Money:** cheap borrowing and more money in the system tend to support risk assets; the opposite hurts them (the macro lessons).
- **Leverage:** debt builds up in good times and unwinds in bad ones.
- **Supply:** in Bitcoin, the reward for creating new coins is cut roughly every four years (the **halving**). Many traders watch the cycles around it. It is a popular idea, but only a handful of cycles exist, so be humble about it.

## What a cycle can and cannot tell you

- It can remind you that **extremes do not last**, and that quiet lows follow loud highs.
- It cannot tell you **when** the turn will come. Many people have called tops and bottoms wrongly for years.
- Each cycle differs in size, speed and causes.

## How to use the idea sensibly

- In euphoria, ask what could go wrong. In despair, ask what is already priced in.
- Size positions smaller late in a long run-up.
- Do not make big, all-in bets based on a cycle theory.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a past cycle as an exact template.
- Buying at the top because "it is a bull market".
- Selling the bottom because "it is a bear market".
- Confusing a pullback with the end of the cycle.
