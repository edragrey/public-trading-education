An **entry** is the rule for **when you open a trade**. Beginners obsess over it. It matters, but less than risk and exits. What it must be is **clear**, so you aren't deciding on a feeling.

## What a written entry has

1. **Context:** the situation where you'll even look (for example, "daily uptrend").
2. **Trigger:** the exact event that says "now".
3. **Order type:** how you'll get in (market or limit).
4. **Stop:** where the idea is wrong, decided **before** you enter.

## Common entry types

- **Breakout:** enter when price moves past a clear level. You get in as the move starts, but false breakouts are common.
- **Pullback:** in an uptrend, enter after a dip that holds above the last swing low. Better price, but it may never pull back.
- **Range edge:** in a sideways market, buy near the bottom of the range and sell near the top. It stops working when the range breaks.
- **Reversal:** trying to catch the turn. Tempting and often the costliest.

> **Hypothetical example.** Context: daily uptrend (higher highs and lows). Trigger: price dips to the 20-day average and closes back above it. Order: buy limit just above that candle's high. Stop: below the swing low. If any of those isn't true, no trade.

## Chasing

Entering after a big move because you can't bear to miss it is the FOMO lesson in action. If the stop would be far from your entry, the trade needs to be small, or you skip it. A distant stop means the position size has to shrink.

## Waiting is part of the entry

Many days won't have a setup. "No trade" is a position. The doing nothing lesson covers why that's a skill.

## Common mistakes

- Entering with no stop in mind.
- Changing the trigger so more trades qualify.
- Entering "a little early" to get a better price, then getting stopped.
- Using five indicators that all need to agree, so you never trade (or force it).

## Try it (no real money)

Write one entry rule in four lines: context, trigger, order type, stop. Scroll through 20 past days on a chart and count how often it would have triggered.

**Key idea:** A good entry is written, specific and includes the stop. It matters, but less than sizing and exits.
