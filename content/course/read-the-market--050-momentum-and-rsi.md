Price can rise quickly or slowly. **Momentum** is how fast and how consistently it's moving. Indicators for it are drawn under the chart.

## What the RSI is

The **Relative Strength Index (RSI)** is a line that moves between **0 and 100**. It compares recent up-moves with recent down-moves, usually over 14 candles.

- Mostly up-moves: the RSI is high.
- Mostly down-moves: it's low.

## The usual reading

- Above **70**: called "overbought".
- Below **30**: called "oversold".

Those names are misleading. They don't mean the price is too high or too low, and they don't mean a reversal is coming. In a strong trend the RSI can stay above 70 for a long time.

> **Hypothetical example.** A coin climbs for two weeks and the RSI sits at 78. A beginner sells because it's "overbought". The coin keeps rising another 30%. The RSI was telling them the move was strong, not that it was over.

## More useful ways to use it

- **Strength of trend:** many traders read RSI staying mostly above 50 as bullish momentum and below 50 as bearish.
- **Divergence:** price makes a new high but the RSI doesn't. It says the push is weakening. It's a clue, not a signal to act on by itself.

## Rules of thumb with honest limits

- RSI is derived from price. It adds no new information; it is a different view.
- It works best as **context**, combined with structure and a stop.
- Like any indicator it's often wrong, which is why you size small and use stops.

## Common mistakes

- Selling just because the RSI hit 70, or buying because it hit 30.
- Hunting for divergences everywhere.
- Using many indicators that all say the same thing.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. What range does the RSI move in?</summary><p>0 to 100.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. Does RSI above 70 mean the price will fall?</summary><p>No. It means recent moves have been mostly up. In strong trends it can stay high.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. What is a divergence?</summary><p>Price makes a new high (or low) but the indicator doesn't, hinting the move is weakening.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. Does an indicator add new information beyond price?</summary><p>No. It re-expresses past prices.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. Why use a stop even with a good-looking indicator signal?</summary><p>Signals are often wrong. The stop limits the loss when they are.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Add the RSI to a chart. Find three times it passed 70 or 30. For each, note what price did over the next ten candles. Write down how often "overbought" was followed by a fall.

**Key idea:** Momentum measures how fast price is moving. RSI is one view of it, and high or low readings are context, not a command.
