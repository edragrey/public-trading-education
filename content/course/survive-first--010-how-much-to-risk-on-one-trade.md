The first lesson showed that one big loss can undo a lot of good forecasting. This lesson gives you the simplest tool to stop that from happening: decide your loss *before* you trade.

## Two words first

**Account** is all the money you've set aside for trading. Not your savings, not your rent: only the part you can afford to lose.

**Risk per trade** is the most you're willing to lose on one trade if it goes wrong. You choose it before you enter.

## The 1% idea

Many careful traders risk about **1% of their account** on any one trade. Some use 0.5%, some use 2%. The exact number matters less than two things: it's **small**, and it's **the same every time**.

> **Hypothetical example.** The account is made up, but the arithmetic is exact.

- Your account is **$2,000**.
- 1% of $2,000 is **$20**.
- So on any single trade, if you're wrong, you lose about **$20**. Not $200. Not "whatever happens".

## Why so small?

Because nobody knows which trades will lose. Even good traders often lose 4, 6 or 10 trades in a row. Small risk turns that bad week into a scratch instead of a wound.

| Risk per trade (hypothetical) | After 10 losses in a row, you have about |
| --- | --- |
| 1% | 90% of your account |
| 2% | 82% |
| 5% | 60% |
| 10% | 35% |

Remember the first lesson: getting back from 35% left (a 65% loss) needs a gain of about **186%**. Getting back from 90% needs about **11%**.

## "But 1% is so little!"

This is the most common pushback, and it's fair. On a $2,000 account, $20 feels like nothing. Two honest answers:

- **It's not the whole trade, it's the loss if you're wrong.** Your trade can be much bigger than $20. The position size lesson shows how.
- **Small risk is what lets you learn.** While you're new, your trades are mostly lessons. Lessons should be cheap.

There's a real cost too: with small risk, your account grows slowly. That's the trade-off, and most experienced traders take it gladly.

## What 1% doesn't do

Being honest about the limits:

- It doesn't make a bad strategy good. It only makes the bad strategy fail slowly, which gives you time to notice.
- It only works if you actually stop at your planned loss. That needs a stop, which is covered in the stops lesson.
- Fees and slippage (see the hidden costs lesson) can make a $20 loss into $22 or $25. Plan for a little extra.

## Common mistakes

- Risking 5 to 10% per trade because you feel sure.
- Changing risk size after wins or losses.
- Calling it too small and thinking it isn't worth it.

## Try it (no real money)

Write down your trading account size, even if it's imaginary. Work out 1% of it. Then write one sentence: "On any one trade, I will lose no more than $___." Put it somewhere you'll see it.

**Key idea:** Decide what you can lose before you think about what you might win. Keep it small and keep it the same.
