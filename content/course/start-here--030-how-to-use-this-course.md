This course is designed to be read in order. Each module gives you a piece of the process, then the later modules use it again.

You do not need to finish everything in one sitting. A short lesson followed by a small practice exercise is more useful than a long session you cannot remember.

## A good rhythm

1. Read one lesson.
2. Open the glossary when a word is unfamiliar.
3. Answer the questions at the bottom without looking first.
4. Write down one idea in your own words.
5. Practise it on a chart or a paper trade, with no real money.

The module quizzes are for checking your understanding, not for proving that you are ready to trade. If an answer surprises you, go back to the lesson behind it.

## Do not skip the boring parts

Beginners often want to jump to chart patterns, leverage or the latest narrative. Those topics can be interesting, but the less exciting skills are the ones that keep a bad trade from becoming a damaging one: deciding your risk, placing an order correctly, recording what happened and reviewing your own behaviour.

The course follows that order deliberately. Mechanics come before tactics. Survival comes before size. A written process comes before confidence.

## What counts as progress

Progress is not finding a trade that went up. It is being able to explain:

- what you expected;
- what could have proved you wrong;
- how much you were willing to lose;
- whether you followed your plan; and
- what you would change next time.

You can practise all of that before you put any money at risk.

**Key idea:** Learn the process first. Keep the cost of being wrong small while you are learning.
