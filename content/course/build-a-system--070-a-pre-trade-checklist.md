Your trade plan says what you're looking for. This checklist makes sure each trade actually matches it. Pilots run through a checklist before every flight, however experienced they are. It's not because they forget how to fly. It's because a checklist catches the mistake you'd make on a bad day.

## One word first

**Gate** here means one yes-or-no question. **Every gate must be a yes.** One no means no trade, however good it looks.

## The six gates

**1. Setup: does this match my written setup?**
Not "sort of". Not "a better version of it". If it's not in the plan, it's not a trade.

**2. Stop: do I know exactly where I'm wrong?**
A specific price, placed where the idea fails (the stops lesson in Survive first). Not a round number that feels okay.

**3. Size: is my loss at the stop within my risk per trade?**
Position size worked out from the stop (the position size lesson in Survive first), including a little extra for fees and slippage. With leverage: does my stop come before liquidation?

**4. Reward: is the target realistic and at least my minimum ratio?**
Somewhere the price has actually turned before, and at least the reward-to-risk your plan asks for (the risk and reward lesson in Survive first).

**5. Conditions: is the market okay to trade right now?**
Enough volume, a normal spread, and no big scheduled announcement about to land (the volume and liquidity lesson, and the news lesson).

**6. Me: am I okay to trade right now?**
Not chasing a loss, not trading because of a post you just saw, not over today's loss limit, not exhausted. The Trader's mind module covers this gate in depth.

## A hypothetical run-through

> **Hypothetical example.** A made-up trade checked against a made-up plan.

| Gate | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Setup | Yes: pullback to a support area in a weekly uptrend |
| 2. Stop | Yes: $47.50, just below the pullback low |
| 3. Size | Yes: $400 position, $20 loss at stop, 1% of account |
| 4. Reward | **No**: the last high is $54, that's only 1.6:1, plan says 2:1 |
| 5. Conditions | Yes |
| 6. Me | Yes |

**Result: no trade.** Five out of six isn't a pass. The trade might still have worked. That's fine. The checklist isn't trying to find every winner; it's trying to keep every trade inside your rules.

## Making it stick

- **Write it on paper or a note** next to your screen.
- **Say the answers out loud** or type them. Thinking "yeah, yeah" doesn't count.
- **Log the "no" trades too.** Over time you'll see whether the checklist is saving you money or missing good trades. That's evidence for the lesson on changing your rules.

## Ready check before your first real trade

This is a suggestion, not a lock. Before putting real money in, you can honestly say:

- Your one-page plan is written, and the checklist above is complete.
- You have logged at least ten paper trades and followed the plan on most of them.
- A daily loss limit is written down.
- You have read the taxes lesson in Going further.
- You know the loss at your stop in dollars, and you accept it.

If one is missing, practise more. The paper trades cost nothing.

## Common mistakes

- Skipping the checklist when you're confident.
- Ticking gates without really checking.
- Changing the gates mid-trade.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. A trade passes five gates out of six. Do you take it?</summary><p>No. Every gate must be a yes.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. Which gate is about you rather than the market?</summary><p>Gate 6. Your state: not chasing losses, not acting on hype, within your daily limit, rested.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. You skipped a trade because it failed a gate, and it would have won. Was the checklist wrong?</summary><p>Not necessarily. The checklist keeps trades inside your rules, not every winner. Log it, and look at many such cases before changing anything.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. What should you do if a gate fails?</summary><p>No trade.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. Why run the checklist even on a trade that looks great?</summary><p>Excitement is when mistakes are most likely.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Write the six gates on a card or a phone note. For the next five pretend trades you consider, run every one through all six and write the answers down.

**Key idea:** Six gates, all yes, or no trade. A checklist catches the mistake you'd make on a bad day.
