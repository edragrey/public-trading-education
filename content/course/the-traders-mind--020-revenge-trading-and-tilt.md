A loss on its own is just a cost of trading. The dangerous part is often what happens **next**. This lesson is about the urge to win it back, straight away.

## Two words first

**Revenge trading** is taking a new trade mainly to win back money you just lost, not because it fits your plan.

**Tilt** is a state where frustration or anger is making your decisions for you. It comes from poker, where players "on tilt" start betting badly after a bad hand.

## How the spiral goes

> **Hypothetical example.** A made-up afternoon. Account $2,000, plan says 1% risk ($20).

1. A trade hits its stop. **−$20.** Annoying, but planned.
2. Straight away, a new trade "to get it back". It's not really a setup. **−$20.**
3. Now down $40. Next trade at **double size** to fix it faster. **−$40.**
4. Down $80. Stop removed "just this once" on the next trade. It falls further. **−$150.**

Total: **−$230**, over 11% of the account. Only the first $20 was part of the plan.

Each step felt reasonable in the moment. That's what tilt does.

## Signs you're on tilt

- You want to trade **right now**, and the reason is a recent loss.
- You're **raising your size** to recover faster.
- You're **moving or removing stops**.
- You feel hot, tense, angry, or you're talking to the screen.
- You're trading a market or setup that isn't in your plan.

## The circuit-breaker

The fix is simple and has to be decided **in advance**, while you're calm:

- **After a loss, wait.** For example: no new trade for 30 minutes, or until the next day.
- **After two (or three) losses in a row, stop for the day.** No exceptions.
- **Stand up.** Physically leave the screen. A walk, a drink of water, anything.

Write your circuit-breaker into your trade plan (see the one-page trade plan lesson in Build a system). The daily loss limit lesson in this module adds a hard stop, which works as a hard stop.

## The money isn't "yours to win back"

A loss is gone. The market doesn't know you lost and owes you nothing. The next trade should be judged exactly like any other: does it pass all six gates? If the honest answer to gate 6 ("am I okay to trade?") is no, then it's no.

## Common mistakes

- Trading straight after a loss.
- Doubling size to win it back.
- Treating the market as an opponent.

## Try it (no real money)

Write your own circuit-breaker: how long you wait after one loss, and after how many losses in a row you stop for the day. Add it to your trade plan.

**Key idea:** The loss that hurts most is often the one you take trying to win back the last one. Decide your circuit-breaker before you need it.
