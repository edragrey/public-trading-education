Trading comes with an endless stream of news, posts, charts and opinions. Most of it will make your trading worse.

## Why too much information hurts

- **It creates FOMO.** Everyone seems to be winning.
- **It pushes you to act** when your plan says wait.
- **It contradicts itself.** For every bullish post there's a bearish one.
- **It's often selling something.** Followers, courses, tokens, signals.
- **It changes your mind** mid-trade for no real reason.

## Sources, ranked roughly

- **Primary data:** a company's filing, an exchange's announcement, a blockchain explorer, an official statement. Closest to the truth.
- **Reputable news:** reporting with named sources and corrections.
- **Analysis and commentary:** useful for ideas, but it's opinion.
- **Social media and chats:** fast and noisy. Treat as rumours until checked.
- **Anonymous accounts and paid promotions:** the lowest trust.

## A simple test for any claim

1. **Who is saying it, and what do they gain?**
2. **Is there a source I can open myself?**
3. **Is it confirmed by at least two independent sources?** If not, call it **unverified**.
4. **Does it ask me to act fast?** Urgency is a warning sign.
5. **Is it about what happened, or a prediction?** Predictions need extra caution.

## Spotting hype and manipulation

- Screenshots of huge gains with no detail.
- "Last chance", "guaranteed", "can't lose".
- Coordinated posts from many accounts at once.
- A call to buy from someone who holds a lot of the thing.
- Anyone who never shows their losses.

## Build a small diet

- Pick **a few** sources you trust and check them at set times.
- Keep a **calendar** of scheduled events.
- **Mute** accounts that make you anxious or greedy.
- **No news in the middle of a trade** unless your plan says so.
- Use the **journal**: write what you read before a trade and whether it helped.

> **Hypothetical example.** A trader checks the calendar and one trusted source each morning, and does nothing else on social media until after the session. They miss some viral moves. They also stop making rushed trades they regret.

## Common mistakes

- Trading on a screenshot.
- Following dozens of accounts that disagree.
- Treating a confident tone as evidence.
- Refreshing a feed mid-trade.

## Try it (no real money)

List every source you read about markets. Mark each as primary, reputable, commentary or noise. Cut the bottom third and write the few you keep, with set times to check them.

**Key idea:** Choose few, reliable sources and check them on a schedule. Treat anything you can't verify as a rumour.
