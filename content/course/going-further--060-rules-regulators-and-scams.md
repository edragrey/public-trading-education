**This is general education, not legal advice.** Rules differ by country and change often. Check your own regulator's website.

## Two words first

- **Regulator:** a public body that sets and enforces rules for financial firms and markets.
- **Licence:** official permission for a firm to offer certain services in a country.

## Who regulates what

Examples, not a full list:

| Place | Examples of regulators | Typical focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| United States | SEC (securities), CFTC (futures) | Stocks, funds, futures, disclosure |
| Spain | CNMV | Securities markets and investment firms |
| European Union | ESMA, and the MiCA rules for crypto | Coordination across countries, crypto-asset rules |

Crypto has been regulated more slowly and unevenly than stocks. Some crypto firms are licensed in one place and not another; some aren't licensed anywhere.

## What a licence does and doesn't mean

- **It can mean:** the firm must follow rules, keep client money separate in some cases, and answer to someone if something goes wrong.
- **It doesn't mean:** the investment will make money, or that you can't lose.
- **Check it yourself:** look the firm up on the regulator's own website. Don't trust a logo on the firm's page.

## Common scams

Scammers target people who are new and hopeful. Typical patterns:

- **Guaranteed returns.** Nobody can promise profit. A promise of safe, high, fixed returns is a warning sign.
- **Pressure and urgency.** "Only today", "spots are limited". Real opportunities don't vanish if you take a day to check.
- **Fake experts and "signals".** Someone offers trading tips, then asks you to pay or to deposit with a particular site.
- **Impersonation.** Messages claiming to be support, a celebrity or a friend, asking for your seed phrase or password.
- **Romance and "mentor" scams.** A new online friend who steers you to invest in a platform you've never heard of.
- **Pump-and-dump.** A crowd hypes a small coin, early buyers sell to the late ones.
- **Recovery scams.** After a loss, someone offers to get your money back for a fee.

> **Hypothetical example.** A stranger messages you with a "trading group" that shows screenshots of big wins and says a platform will double your deposit in a week. You deposit $200, and the balance on screen grows. When you try to withdraw, you're asked for a "tax fee". The balance was never real.

## Simple rules to stay safe

1. **Never share** your seed phrase, private key or password with anyone, ever. Real support never asks.
2. **Check licences** on the regulator's own site.
3. **Search the name plus "scam" or "withdrawal problems"** before depositing.
4. **Test with a small amount** and try a withdrawal early.
5. **If it sounds too good, walk away.**

## Common mistakes

- Trusting a firm because its website looks professional.
- Following signals from people you've never met.
- Paying a fee to "unlock" your own money.

## Try it (no real money)

Pick a well-known exchange or broker. Find its regulator on the regulator's own website. Write down what it is licensed for and where, and search for the firm's name with "complaints".

**Key idea:** Regulators set rules, but a licence is not a guarantee. Verify it yourself, never share your seed phrase, and treat any promise of guaranteed profit as a warning.
