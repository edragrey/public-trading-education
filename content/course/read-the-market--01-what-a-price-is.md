Welcome to the second module. Survive first was about protecting your money. This one is about understanding what you're looking at when you open a chart. We start with the smallest piece: a single price.

## A price is a deal

The price you see for a stock or a coin is simply **the last price at which someone bought and someone else sold**. That's all. It isn't a fair value, a forecast or an opinion. It's the most recent deal.

## Two words first

**Bid** is the highest price any buyer is currently offering.

**Ask** (or offer) is the lowest price any seller is currently willing to accept.

The gap between them is the **spread**, which you met in the fees lesson.

## The order book

Every exchange keeps a list of everyone waiting to buy and sell, at what price and how much. That list is the **order book**.

> **Hypothetical example.** A made-up order book for a made-up coin.

| Waiting to buy (bids) | Price | Waiting to sell (asks) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| | $100.20 | 15 coins |
| | $100.10 | 8 coins |
| | $100.05 | 5 coins |
| 6 coins | $100.00 | |
| 10 coins | $99.90 | |
| 20 coins | $99.80 | |

- The best bid is **$100.00**. The best ask is **$100.05**. The spread is **5 cents**.
- If you want to buy **right now**, you pay the ask: **$100.05**.
- If you want to sell **right now**, you get the bid: **$100.00**.

## Two kinds of order

**Market order** says "buy (or sell) now, at the best price available". It fills straight away, and takes from the book.

**Limit order** says "buy (or sell) only at this price or better". It waits in the book until someone takes it, and it may never fill.

## How a price moves, one deal at a time

Using the book above: someone sends a market order to buy **10 coins**.

1. They take the **5 coins** at $100.05.
2. Then **5** of the 8 coins at $100.10.
3. The last deal happened at **$100.10**, so that's the new price on your screen.

Nothing "happened" in the news. A buyer was simply more eager than the sellers waiting, and used up the cheapest coins on offer. That is how every price move works, big or small.

## Why this matters to you

- **Bigger orders move the price more** when the book is thin. That's slippage, from Survive first.
- **The chart price isn't always the price you'll get.** You buy at the ask and sell at the bid.
- In quiet markets, or for small coins, the spread can be wide. That's a cost every time.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. The best bid is $50.00 and the best ask is $50.10. You place a market order to buy. Roughly what do you pay?</summary><p>About $50.10, the ask. Possibly a little more if your order is bigger than what's waiting at that price.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. What's the difference between a market order and a limit order?</summary><p>A market order fills now at the best price available. A limit order only fills at your price or better, and may not fill at all.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. Does a price move always mean there was news?</summary><p>No. Every move is just buyers and sellers making deals. Sometimes one side is simply more eager.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Many exchanges and broker apps let you view the order book without logging in or trading. Open one for any coin or stock. Find the best bid, the best ask, and work out the spread.

**Key idea:** A price is just the last deal. Behind it there are always two prices, the bid and the ask, and a queue of people waiting.
