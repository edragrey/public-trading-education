Many traders step away after a big loss, or a long losing period, and then come back. Returning is allowed. How you return decides whether you rebuild or repeat.

## Two words first

A **drawdown** is the fall from your highest account value to a later low. A 30% drawdown means you are 30% below your peak.

**Rebuilding** means returning to trading carefully, with the aim of rebuilding your **process** first and your account second.

## Start by being honest

Before trading again, ask:

- Why did I lose? Was it the rules, the size, or breaking my own rules?
- Is my plan written down, and would I follow it?
- Am I coming back for good reasons, or to "get it back"?

If the honest answer is "to win it back", that is the revenge trading lesson. Wait.

## Experience is not an edge

You may have traded for years. That helps you **notice** things. It does not mean the market owes you anything. Markets change, and old habits can come back fast. Treat the return as a restart, with a clear head and a smaller size.

## A ladder back

> **Hypothetical example.** After a long break you decide on four steps. You move up only after a set number of trades that **followed your rules**, not after profit.
> 1. **Paper trade** for several weeks. No real money.
> 2. **Very small real size**, for example a quarter of your old risk per trade.
> 3. **Half size** after 20 rule-following trades.
> 4. **Normal size** only after another stretch of rule-following trades.

If you break a rule or hit a daily or weekly loss limit, step **down** one rung. The point is to rebuild trust in your own process.

## Don't chase the number

If you are down 50%, you need +100% to recover (the percentages lesson). Trying to do that fast means big risks and bigger losses. The slow way is usually the only reliable way.

## Look after the person, too

- Money you cannot afford to lose should not be in trading.
- If trading is hurting your sleep, your relationships or your mood, take a break or talk to someone.
- It is fine to decide trading is not for you right now.

## Common mistakes

- Returning with full size on day one.
- Trading bigger to recover faster.
- Assuming past skill means no process is needed.
- Skipping the written plan because "I know what to do".

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. What do you rebuild first?</summary><p>Your process, then your account.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. Is experience an edge?</summary><p>No. It helps you notice things, but the market owes you nothing.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. What do you do before real money?</summary><p>Paper trade for a while to practise following your plan.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. When do you move up a rung?</summary><p>After a set number of rule-following trades, not just after profit.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. What do you do if you break a rule?</summary><p>Step down one rung.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Write your own ladder: the sizes, and how many rule-following trades you need before each step up.

**Key idea:** rebuild slowly, with smaller size and paper trading first, and move up only on following your rules.
