You've learned to limit the risk on one trade. But what if you hold five trades that all lose for the same reason?

## Diversification

**Diversification** means not putting all your money in one thing. If one position fails, the others can cushion it.

## Correlation

**Correlation** describes how much two things move together.

- **High (positive):** when one goes up, the other usually does too. Many coins move this way, especially in a crash.
- **Low:** they move more independently.
- **Negative:** they tend to move in opposite directions.

The idea is simple, but it hides a trap: **five positions with high correlation behave like one big position.**

> **Hypothetical example.** You hold five different small coins and risk 1% on each, so 5% total. A market-wide sell-off hits, and all five fall to their stops together. You lose about 5%, not 1%. That's one bet repeated five times.

## Where hidden concentration comes from

- **Same market:** most crypto coins fall together in a big move.
- **Same theme:** several coins from one narrative (for example one hot sector) are one idea in disguise.
- **Same direction:** five long positions in a market that suddenly drops.
- **Same currency:** many assets are priced in dollars and respond to the same news.
- **Leverage:** borrowing amplifies every one of them (see the leverage lessons).

## How to limit it

1. **Count open risk, not trades.** Add up the risk of all open positions. Many traders cap the total (for example 3 to 5% of the account).
2. **Group by theme.** Treat similar positions as one when you count.
3. **Mix directions or markets** only if you really understand both.
4. **Check in a stress test:** "If everything fell the same day, how much would I lose?"
5. **Keep some cash.** Not being invested is a position too.

## What diversification can't do

- It can't remove market-wide risk. In a crash, correlations often rise toward 1, so things you thought were different fall together.
- Too much spreading can mean you can't follow any of it. Ten positions you can't watch is worse than three you understand.

**See it happen.** When everything falls together: [Read the March 2020 case study](/course/going-further/case-study-march-2020/).

## Common mistakes

- Counting coins instead of counting risk.
- Thinking different names mean different risks.
- Adding a position to "balance" without checking what it correlates with.
- Ignoring that your whole account is exposed to one platform.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. What does high correlation mean?</summary><p>Two things tend to move in the same direction at the same time.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. You risk 1% on each of 4 highly correlated trades. Roughly how much could one bad day cost?</summary><p>Close to 4%, since they may all hit their stops together.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. Why do correlations often rise in a crash?</summary><p>Fear makes people sell almost everything, so assets fall together.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. What's better than counting positions?</summary><p>Counting total open risk across positions.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. Can you be too diversified?</summary><p>Yes. Too many positions to follow means you can't manage any of them well.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

List your current (or imagined) positions. Group them by theme and market. Add up the risk in each group. Write the total if everything went wrong together.

**Key idea:** Several positions that move together are one big bet. Count total open risk, not the number of trades.
