Willpower is weakest exactly when you need it most: after losses, when you're tired, frustrated or desperate to turn the day around. A daily loss limit replaces willpower with a rule you set while calm.

## Two words first

**Daily loss limit** is the most you allow yourself to lose in one day. Reach it, and you stop trading until tomorrow. No exceptions.

**Weekly loss limit** works the same way, over a week.

## Why it works

Survive first showed that small losses are easy to recover from and big ones aren't. The revenge trading lesson showed that the biggest losses usually come from a spiral, not one trade. A daily limit **cuts the spiral off** before it can get deep.

> **Hypothetical example.** A made-up account of $2,000, risking 1% ($20) per trade, with a daily limit of 3% ($60).

- Loss one: −$20. Loss two: −$20. Loss three: −$20. **Limit reached. Stop.**
- Worst possible day: about **−3%**. Easily recovered.
- Without the limit, the revenge spiral from the revenge trading lesson cost **over 11%** in one afternoon.

## Setting your limits

Common starting points (pick your own, and write them down):

| Risk per trade | A typical daily limit | A typical weekly limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.5% | 1.5% | 3% |
| 1% | 2% to 3% | 5% to 6% |
| 2% | 4% | 8% |

A simple rule of thumb: **daily limit = 2 or 3 losing trades**. Weekly = about twice the daily.

## Making it real

- **Write it in your trade plan** (line 9 of the one-page plan in Build a system).
- **Count open risk too.** If a stop on a trade you're still in would take you past the limit, don't open another one.
- **Use platform tools** if they exist. Some brokers and exchanges let you set loss limits or "cool-off" periods that lock you out.
- **When you hit it, leave.** Close the app. Don't "just watch". Watching leads to trading.

## A limit is a success, not a failure

Hitting your daily limit means the system did its job: it stopped a bad day from becoming a disaster. Log it in your journal as "limit reached, stopped" and be proud of that line.

## Common mistakes

- Setting a limit and breaking it.
- Setting it so wide it never triggers.
- Feeling a limit means failure.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. You risk 1% per trade with a 3% daily limit. You've lost three trades today. What now?</summary><p>Stop for the day. You've hit your limit.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. Why is a daily limit better than "I'll stop when it feels like a bad day"?</summary><p>Because after losses, your feelings push you to keep trading. A rule set in advance doesn't change with your mood.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. You're at −2.5% with a 3% limit, and the next trade risks 1%. Should you take it?</summary><p>No. If it hits the stop, you'd be at −3.5%, past your limit.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. What do you do when you reach your daily limit?</summary><p>Stop trading for the day.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. Why decide the limit before the day starts?</summary><p>Because you won't judge it well after losing.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Choose your daily and weekly limits, write them on line 9 of your trade plan, and check whether your platform has a loss-limit or cool-off feature.

**Key idea:** Decide how much you can lose in a day before the day starts. When you reach it, stop. That's the system working.
