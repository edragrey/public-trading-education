This course is about trading, but it would be unfair not to say that for many people a slower approach fits better.

## Trading vs investing

| | Trading | Investing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time frame | Days to weeks (sometimes less) | Years to decades |
| Goal | Profit from price moves | Grow money with the economy and businesses |
| Effort | High: plans, charts, journal | Low to medium: set up, then check occasionally |
| Costs | Higher (many trades) | Lower (few trades) |
| Typical result | Wide range, many beginners lose | Tracks the market, with big swings |

Neither is "better". They are different jobs.

## What an index fund and an ETF are

An **index** is a list of many companies, for example the biggest 500 in a country. An **index fund** holds all of them in one product, so you own a tiny slice of each. An **ETF** (exchange-traded fund) is a fund you buy and sell on an exchange like a share.

- You spread your money across many companies in one go.
- Fees are often low.
- You get the market's return, minus the fund's fee, not more.

> **Hypothetical example.** Instead of choosing one company, you buy an ETF that holds hundreds. If one fails, it's a small piece of the whole. If the whole market falls, you fall too.

## Things to know about funds

- **Fees:** the yearly cost is called the expense ratio. Small differences add up over decades.
- **What's inside matters.** "Diversified" means different things for different funds.
- **They can still fall a lot.** A fund tracking the market falls when the market does.
- **Some products are complex or leveraged.** Read what it holds before buying.

## Regular investing

Many people invest a fixed amount on a schedule, for example monthly. It's called **dollar-cost averaging**. You buy more units when prices are low and fewer when high. It doesn't guarantee a profit. It does remove the need to guess the timing.

## How to decide

Ask yourself:

1. **Do I have the time and interest to trade properly?** It's closer to a skilled hobby or job than passive income.
2. **Is it money I can afford to lose?** Money for the next few years of your life shouldn't be traded.
3. **Do I already have an emergency fund?** Build that first.
4. **Would I be happy if my results just matched the market?** If so, investing in an index may be enough.

Many people do both: a **long-term core** in diversified funds, and a **small trading account** to learn and practise with an amount they can afford to lose.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a trading account as your retirement plan.
- Chasing last year's best fund.
- Ignoring fees.
- Trading so often that costs and taxes eat the gains.
