## 1. A price rises from $50 to $60. What is the percent change?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="b">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">+10%</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">+20%</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">+16.7%</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>The move is $10, divided by the starting $50 = 20%.</p>
</div>

## 2. You lose 50% of your account. What gain gets you back to even?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="c">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">+50%</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">+75%</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">+100%</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>Half of $100 is $50. Doubling $50 is a 100% gain.</p>
</div>

## 3. +10% then −10% on $100 leaves you with:

<div class="quiz" data-answer="b">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">$100</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">$99</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">$101</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>$110 minus 10% of $110 ($11) = $99. You divide by the new, bigger number.</p>
</div>

## 4. What does 1R mean?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="b">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">$1 of profit</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">The amount you risked on that trade</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">A 1% move</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>R is your risk on the trade, used as a ruler so trades of different sizes can be compared.</p>
</div>

## 5. You risk $30 and make $75. How many R is that?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="a">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">+2.5R</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">+2R</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">+45R</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>$75 ÷ $30 = 2.5R.</p>
</div>

## 6. What makes compounding work against you?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="a">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">Big losses and big swings</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">Small steady gains</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">Holding for long</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>Losses apply to the current amount too, and swings drag the result down.</p>
</div>

## 7. Account $4,000, risk 1%, stop 4% away. What position size?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="a">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">$1,000</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">$4,000</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">$160</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>Risk = $40. $40 ÷ 4% = $1,000.</p>
</div>

## 8. Why must a price rise a little just to break even?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="b">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">Taxes are added automatically</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">Fees are paid on entry and exit</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">The market always drops first</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>You pay costs both ways, so the price has to cover them.</p>
</div>

## 9. On a log chart, equal vertical distances show:

<div class="quiz" data-answer="b">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">Equal dollar moves</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">Equal percent moves</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">Equal time</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>Log scales space percent steps equally, so +100% looks the same wherever it happens.</p>
</div>

## 10. A trader wins 7 of 10 trades. What can you say?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="b">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">They have a proven edge</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">It is a small sample and could be luck</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">They should double their size</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>Someone with no edge does this about once in six tries. You need many more trades.</p>
</div>
