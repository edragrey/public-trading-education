"It's only one cent, imagine if it goes to a dollar!" This is one of the most common beginner thoughts in crypto. This lesson explains why the price of a single coin tells you almost nothing about whether it's cheap or expensive.

## Words first

**Market cap** (market capitalisation) is the total value of everything that's out there: **price × number of coins (or shares) in circulation**.

**Circulating supply** is how many coins are actually out in the market now.

**Total or maximum supply** is how many will exist eventually, including ones not released yet.

**Fully diluted value (FDV)** is the price × the maximum supply: what the whole thing would be valued at if every coin were already out.

**Unlock** is when coins that were held back (often for the team, early investors or rewards) are released and can be sold.

## Price alone is meaningless

> **Hypothetical example.** Two made-up coins.

| | Coin A | Coin B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | $0.01 | $50 |
| Circulating supply | 500 billion | 10 million |
| Market cap | **$5 billion** | **$500 million** |

Coin A looks cheap at one cent. But the market already values it at **ten times** coin B. For coin A to reach $1, its market cap would have to become **$500 billion**, bigger than most companies on Earth.

## Stocks work the same way

A share priced at $500 isn't more "expensive" than one priced at $5. What matters is the company's total value compared with what it earns and owns. Sites that list companies by market cap show this side by side.

## Supply that hasn't arrived yet

Here's where FDV matters. If a coin has only **20%** of its supply circulating, the other **80%** will arrive over time. Every unlock adds coins that their owners may want to sell.

This doesn't mean the price must fall on an unlock day. Sometimes unlocks are expected and already reflected in the price. But **more supply needs more buyers just to keep the price steady**. A big gap between market cap and FDV is worth knowing about before you buy.

## Where to find this

Most crypto data sites show circulating supply, maximum supply, market cap and FDV for each coin, and many show an unlock schedule. The project's own documents usually list who holds what and when it unlocks. Treat any single source as a starting point, and check a second.

## Try it (no real money)

Pick a coin you've heard of. Find its price, circulating supply and maximum supply. Use the calculator to work out the market cap, the FDV and how much is still to be released.

**Key idea:** A single price tells you nothing about value. Look at the market cap, and at how much supply is still on its way.
