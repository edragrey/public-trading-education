A big loss can feel like a punch. It's also one of the most important moments in your trading life, because what you do next matters more than the loss.

## The first hour: stop and breathe

1. **Stop trading.** Close the chart. The urge to win it back right away is the revenge trading lesson in full force.
2. **Check the damage honestly.** Look at the numbers once, then put the screen away.
3. **Don't make big decisions.** No deposits, no new positions, no quitting forever, no telling the world.
4. **Move.** Walk, eat, sleep. Your body affects your judgment.

## The first day: write down what happened

When you're calmer, write it plainly:

- What did I plan to do?
- What did I actually do?
- Was this a loss within my rules, or did I break them?

There are two very different kinds of big loss:

- **A loss inside the rules** (the stop worked, or a gap). Painful but it's the cost of the game. Nothing to fix except perhaps size.
- **A loss from breaking the rules** (moved a stop, oversized, chased). Here you've learned something real. Write down the exact rule you broke and what led to it.

## The first week: reduce size, not hope

- **Cut your size** (many traders halve it) or go back to paper trading until you've had a few rule-following trades.
- **Re-read your plan.** Does it need clarifying, or do you need to follow it?
- **Don't chase the number.** Needing +25% to recover from -20% is a real drawback (see the losing streaks lesson). Slow and steady is the way back.
- **Talk to someone** you trust, or write. Hiding it makes it heavier.

## Feelings you may have

- **Shame:** nearly every trader has been here. It's not a verdict on you.
- **Anger:** at the market, at yourself. Let it pass before you trade.
- **Numbness or loss of interest:** a sign to take a proper break.
- **Urgency:** the voice that says "do something now". That's the most dangerous one.

If a loss is affecting your sleep, relationships or sense of worth, step away from trading and talk to someone you trust. Money can be rebuilt. You matter more than an account.

## When to come back

Come back when:

- you can read your plan without feeling sick,
- you've written what went wrong,
- you'll trade at reduced size,
- and you have a daily and weekly loss limit written down.

## Common mistakes

- Trading bigger to recover.
- Ignoring the loss and carrying on as if nothing happened.
- Blaming the market for a rule break.
- Telling yourself you'll never trade again, then returning angry.
