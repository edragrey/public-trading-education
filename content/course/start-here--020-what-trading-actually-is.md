At its simplest, trading means exchanging something because you think its value will change. You buy and later sell, or you use a contract that gains or loses as the price moves.

The difficult part is that nobody knows the next price with certainty. Even a sensible idea can be wrong. Even a correct idea can arrive at the wrong time, or take a path that you cannot tolerate.

That means every trade is a small decision under uncertainty.

## The questions inside a trade

Before a trade exists, someone has to answer:

1. **What am I trading?** A stock, coin, commodity, fund or contract?
2. **Why this one?** What is the evidence, rather than the hope?
3. **What would make me enter?** What condition has to be true first?
4. **How much can I lose?** This is different from asking how much money to put in.
5. **Where is the idea wrong?** What observation or price would invalidate it?
6. **What will I do next?** Before the market moves, decide how you will handle a win, a loss and a market that goes nowhere.

You do not need perfect answers. You do need answers that are clear enough to write down and review later.

## Trading is not the same as investing

Investing usually starts with a longer-term belief about an asset, a business or an economy. Trading usually starts with a defined setup, a time horizon and a plan for what happens if the setup fails.

The boundary is not perfectly sharp. Someone can invest in a stock and trade a crypto position. The useful question is: **what is my plan, and when will I review it?**

## Start with practice

You can learn the structure of a trade without risking money. Pick a liquid market, write down an entry, an invalidation point and a reason, then follow what happened. That is a paper trade. It gives you something to review before money and emotion make the lesson more expensive.

**Key idea:** A trade is a plan for uncertainty, not a promise that your forecast will come true.
