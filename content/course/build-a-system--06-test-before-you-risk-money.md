You've written a plan. Before you risk real money on it, it makes sense to check whether it holds up at all. This lesson covers three cheap ways to test, and is honest about what each one misses.

## A few words first

**Backtest** means checking how your rules would have done on past charts.

**Paper trading** means following your rules in real time, but with pretend money. Many platforms offer a demo account for this.

**Sample size** is how many trades you've looked at. Bigger samples are more trustworthy.

## Way 1: look back (manual backtest)

Scroll back through charts of the market you trade. Every time your setup appears, note where you'd have entered, stopped and taken profit, using only what you could have seen at the time. Write each one in your journal format, in R.

**Good for:** quickly seeing if the setup appears often, and if the numbers aren't obviously terrible.

**Watch out for:** hindsight. It's very easy to "see" setups that were only obvious afterwards, or skip the ugly ones. Be strict with yourself.

## Way 2: paper trade forward

Follow your plan in real time with a demo account or on paper, for a set number of trades, say 30. Use the full checklist and journal.

**Good for:** testing whether you can **actually follow** the plan in real time, and whether the setup still works in today's market.

**Watch out for:** paper trades don't feel like real ones. There's no fear of losing, so it's easier to follow the rules. Fills can also look better than reality.

## Way 3: go small with real money

Trade the plan with real money, but at a **fraction** of your normal risk, for example 0.25% instead of 1%.

**Good for:** adding real emotions and real costs (fees, spread, slippage) without much damage.

**Watch out for:** small stakes can make you careless. Treat every trade as if it were full size.

## How many trades is enough?

There's no magic number, but **10 is far too few** and **30 is a sensible minimum** before drawing even rough conclusions. Expectancy (Survive first, lesson 6) jumps around a lot in small samples.

> **Hypothetical example.** A made-up setup that, over the long run, wins about 40% at 2:1. In the first 10 trades it could easily win only 2 (looking terrible) or 6 (looking brilliant) by chance alone. Only over many trades does the real picture settle.

## Moving up in steps

A common path: look back → paper trade 30 trades → real money at small size for 30 trades → normal size. Move up a step only if you **followed the plan** and the results are at least reasonable. Drop back a step if either fails.

## Try it (no real money)

Take your plan from lesson 2. Find 10 past examples of your setup on charts, being strict about hindsight. Record each one in R. It's a start, not a verdict.

**Key idea:** Test cheaply before you risk real money: look back, paper trade, then go small. And don't judge anything on 10 trades.
