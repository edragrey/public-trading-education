Surviving isn't only about bad trades. A lot of beginners lose money to scams, fake "gurus" and stolen accounts before they ever place a real trade. This lesson covers the common traps and a simple checklist.

## A few words first

**Scam** is any scheme designed to take your money by lying to you.

**Seed phrase** (or recovery phrase) is the list of 12 or 24 words that controls a crypto wallet. Whoever has it controls the money.

**Two-factor authentication (2FA)** is a second check when you log in, like a code from an app on your phone, on top of your password.

## Red flags

If you see any of these, slow down:

- **Guaranteed returns.** "10% a week, guaranteed." Nobody can guarantee returns. Real markets go down too.
- **Urgency.** "Only 2 hours left!" Pressure to act fast is a classic way to stop you thinking.
- **Someone asks for your seed phrase or password.** No real exchange, wallet or support team will ever ask for it. Ever.
- **Unsolicited messages.** A stranger in your DMs offering to "help" you trade, or a "support agent" who contacts you first.
- **Paid signal groups with screenshots of wins.** Screenshots are easy to fake, and losses are rarely shown.
- **"Send me crypto and I'll send back double."** Always a scam, even when it seems to come from a famous person.

## Common scams in crypto

- **Fake support.** You post a problem online, and "support" messages you with a link. The link steals your login or seed phrase.
- **Rug pulls.** A new token is hyped, people buy in, and the creators sell everything and disappear.
- **Pump and dumps.** A group buys a small coin, hypes it loudly, then sells to the people who rushed in.
- **Fake websites and apps.** A copy of a real exchange site with one letter changed in the address.
- **Romance and "friend" scams.** A slow-building relationship that ends with an "investment opportunity".

## Hype isn't a scam, but it can still hurt

Not every loud coin is a scam. But hype makes prices move fast in both directions, often drawing beginners in near the top. If you notice you want to buy *because* everyone's talking about it, that's a signal to pause, not to click. The Read the market module looks at hype cycles, and the Trader's mind module looks at FOMO.

## Your safety checklist

1. **Turn on 2FA** with an authenticator app (not just SMS) on every exchange and email account.
2. **Never share your seed phrase or password** with anyone, for any reason. Store the seed phrase offline.
3. **Type exchange addresses yourself** or use a saved bookmark. Don't click links in messages or ads.
4. **Start small** with any new platform. Send a little first and make sure you can withdraw it.
5. **Keep trading money separate** from savings, and only use money you can afford to lose.
6. **If it sounds too good to be true, it is.**

## Common mistakes

- Clicking links in messages or ads instead of typing the address.
- Sharing a recovery phrase with anyone, including 'support'.
- Skipping two-factor authentication.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. A support agent messages you first and asks for your 12 words to "fix your wallet". What do you do?</summary><p>Refuse and block. No real support team ever needs your seed phrase. Anyone who has it can take your money.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. What's a safe way to try a new exchange?</summary><p>Start with a small amount and check you can withdraw it before sending more.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. Someone messages you offering to double your crypto. What do you do?</summary><p>Ignore and block. Doubling offers are a classic scam.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. Where should a recovery phrase be stored?</summary><p>Offline, on paper, never in a photo, chat or cloud note.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Spend ten minutes on your accounts: turn on app-based 2FA everywhere you can, bookmark the real addresses of any exchange you use, and check where your seed phrase is kept.

**Key idea:** The easiest money to lose is money you never got to trade with. Protect your accounts before you protect your trades.
