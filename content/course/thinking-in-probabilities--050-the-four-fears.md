Fear isn't a flaw. It's an alarm. The trouble starts when it quietly takes over decisions. Four fears show up again and again.

## 1. Fear of being wrong

Being wrong feels like a judgement on you. So people hold losers ("it will come back") or close winners quickly to lock in being right.

**Spot it:** you can't take a small loss, or you cut winners at tiny profits.

## 2. Fear of losing money

Heavy losses make people so cautious they hesitate on valid setups, or freeze when a stop should be used.

**Spot it:** you skip good setups right after a loss, or you hover over the exit button.

## 3. Fear of missing out

Watching a move without you feels like losing something. So you chase, late and too big.

**Spot it:** you enter after a big candle, with no plan, because "it's going without me".

## 4. Fear of leaving money on the table

You exit a winner on plan, then it keeps going, and you feel cheated. The next time you hold too long or skip the target.

**Spot it:** you change your exit rule after one missed run.

## What helps

- **Name the fear.** "This is fear of missing out" takes away some of its power.
- **Shrink the size** until the fear quiets down.
- **Return to the plan:** what do my rules say?
- **Record it** in your journal. Patterns show up quickly in writing.

## Common mistakes

- Reading fear as a signal that your plan is wrong.
- Changing the exit rule after one missed move.
- Hiding fear-driven trades from your journal.
