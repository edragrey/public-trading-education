Mark Douglas sets out a handful of basic truths about markets. Here they are in our own words, with a simple example for each.

## 1. Anything can happen

No matter how good a setup looks, price can do something unexpected. A surprise announcement, a big order, a sudden mood shift.

**Use it:** always have a stop. Treat "it can't fall further" as a thought, not a fact.

## 2. You don't need to know what happens next to make money

You need an edge and good risk control. The casino analogy again: it makes money without predicting any single hand.

**Use it:** stop hunting for certainty. Look for a repeatable plan.

## 3. Wins and losses are spread out at random

Even with a real edge, winners and losers arrive in an order you can't predict. Three losses in a row can happen right before a big winner.

**Use it:** expect streaks. Don't change a good plan because of a short run.

## 4. An edge is just a higher chance of one outcome

It's a tilt, not a guarantee. 55% is still 45% the other way.

**Use it:** judge the plan over a series. A single loss doesn't disprove it.

## 5. Every moment in the market is unique

Two charts can look alike, yet the people, news and money behind them differ. Patterns repeat in a rough way, not in an exact way.

**Use it:** stay flexible, size small and let the stop do its job.

## Why accepting beats agreeing

Many people nod along to these. Acceptance is different: it shows up in what you do after a loss. If a stop-out still feels like a personal failure, the truth hasn't sunk in yet. That's normal, and it improves with practice.

## Common mistakes

- Agreeing with the ideas but not changing behaviour.
- Hunting for a setup that can't fail.
- Dropping a sound plan after a short bad run.
