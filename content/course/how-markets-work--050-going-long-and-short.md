Most people think trading only means buying and hoping it goes up. You can also take a position that gains when the price **falls**.

## Long: you gain when the price rises

Going **long** means buying. You hold something that you want to be worth more.

> **Hypothetical example.** You buy 10 coins at $100 and sell at $110. You gained $10 per coin, so **$100**. If it falls to $90 you've lost **$100**.

Your most you can lose on a plain long (no leverage) is what you paid. A coin can't go below $0.

## Short: you gain when the price falls

Going **short** means selling something you don't own yet, then buying it back later, hopefully cheaper.

How it works with a borrowed asset: you borrow 10 coins and sell them at $100 (you get $1,000). If the price falls to $90, you buy 10 coins for $900 and return them. You keep the **$100** difference.

> **Hypothetical example.** Same trade, but it rises to $110 instead. Buying back costs $1,100, so you lose **$100**. If it rose to $200, you'd lose $1,000.

## Why short is riskier

- **A long can fall to zero; a short has no ceiling.** A price can rise without limit, so a loss can in theory grow without limit.
- **Borrowing costs.** You often pay a fee or funding rate to hold a short.
- **Squeezes.** If many shorts are forced to buy back at once, the price can spike, which forces more buying.
- **Not always available.** Some assets or brokers don't allow shorting.

That's why stops matter even more when short, and why most beginners should learn long first.

## Shorting in practice

You usually don't borrow coins by hand. Common routes:

- **Margin trading** on an exchange, borrowing to short.
- **Perpetual futures** or **futures**, a contract that gains when the price falls. (Covered in a later lesson.)
- **Inverse products** that rise when the market falls.

## Common mistakes

- Shorting just because "it can't go higher".
- Using no stop on a short.
- Ignoring funding or borrowing fees.
- Calling a market "overvalued" and treating that as a reason to short. Markets can stay overvalued for a long time.

## Try it (no real money)

In a paper trading account, open one hypothetical long and one hypothetical short on the same asset with the same size. Move the price in your head by 5% up and 5% down and write down the result for each.

**Key idea:** Long gains when price rises and short gains when it falls. A long can lose at most what you put in; a short can lose more, so it needs extra care.
