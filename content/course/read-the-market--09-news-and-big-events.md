Some days, a single announcement moves the whole market in seconds. This lesson covers the main kinds of events, why reactions can be surprising, and the simple ways careful traders protect themselves around them.

## Scheduled events

Many big market movers happen on known dates, published in advance on an **economic calendar** (a free list of upcoming announcements):

- **Interest rate decisions.** Central banks like the US Federal Reserve or the European Central Bank decide what borrowing costs. Higher rates tend to pull money away from riskier things.
- **Inflation figures.** For example the US CPI (consumer price index), which measures how fast prices in shops are rising. It affects what people expect central banks to do.
- **Jobs reports.** How many people found work. Another clue about the economy and rates.
- **Company earnings.** For stocks, the quarterly report of how much a company sold and earned.
- **Crypto-specific events.** Token unlocks (lesson 7), network upgrades, or new rules from regulators.

## Unscheduled events

Hacks, a company collapsing, a sudden regulation, a war. These can't be planned for, which is one of the reasons Survive first insists on stops and small risk on every trade.

## "Good news, price down?"

This confuses almost everyone at first. A company reports record profits and its share price falls. How?

> **Hypothetical example.** A made-up company.

- Before the report, most people expect profits of **$1.20** per share. The price already reflects that hope.
- The company reports **$1.10**. That's a record, but it's **less than expected**.
- The price falls.

Markets react to **news compared with what was expected**, not to news on its own. "Better than feared" can push a price up even when the news sounds bad. This is often called "priced in": if everyone already expected it, there's little left to react to.

## What happens around big events

- **Fast moves in both directions.** Prices can jump one way, then reverse within minutes.
- **Wider spreads and more slippage** (Survive first, lesson 8), because many orders get pulled just before the announcement.
- **Stops can fill far away** from where you set them.

## Staying safe around announcements

Common habits careful traders use:

- **Check the calendar** each week, so nothing important surprises you.
- **Don't open new trades just before** a big scheduled event, especially as a beginner.
- **Make positions smaller** if you're holding through one, so a gap or slippage can't hurt much.
- **Wait for the dust to settle.** The first minutes after an announcement are often the noisiest.
