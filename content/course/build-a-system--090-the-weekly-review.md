A journal on its own is just a pile of notes. Once a week, you sit down and read it. That's the **weekly review**: the moment your trades turn into lessons.

## One word first

**Review** here means looking back over the week's trades with fresh eyes, outside market hours, to spot patterns. It's not about the result. It's about how you got it.

## The 20-minute routine

Pick a fixed time when markets are quiet for you, like Sunday morning. Then go through five questions.

**1. The numbers (5 minutes).**
How many trades? Total result in R? Biggest win and loss in R? Any loss bigger than −1R?

**2. Did I follow the plan? (5 minutes)**
Count the trades where you answered "no". Add up their results separately. This is usually the most useful number of the week.

**3. Patterns (5 minutes).**
Anything that keeps coming up? Same time of day, same mood, same mistake, same market?

**4. One thing to keep, one thing to change (3 minutes).**
Just one of each. Not a list.

**5. Next week (2 minutes).**
Any big scheduled events (see the news and big events lesson)? Any rule you'll pay special attention to?

## A hypothetical review

> **Hypothetical example.** A made-up week for a made-up beginner.

- **Numbers:** 6 trades, total −1.4R. One loss of −1.8R.
- **Plan followed:** 4 trades yes (total +0.9R), 2 trades no (total −2.3R).
- **Pattern:** both rule-breaks happened within an hour of a loss.
- **Keep:** waiting for the daily close before entering. **Change:** after a loss, no new trade until the next day.
- **Next week:** interest rate decision on Wednesday; no new trades that day.

The week was down. But the system's trades were up, and the whole loss came from two impulsive trades. That's a very different lesson from "my strategy doesn't work".

## Don't overreact to one week

One week is a tiny sample. A bad week **following your plan** is not a reason to change the plan. A good week **breaking your plan** is not a reason to keep breaking it. The lesson on changing your rules covers when changes are justified.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. Your week was down overall, but every trade that followed your plan was up. What's the lesson?</summary><p>The losses came from breaking the plan. Fix the behaviour, not the system.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. How many things should you change after a weekly review?</summary><p>One. Changing many things at once makes it impossible to tell what helped.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. You had a great week by ignoring your stop rules. Should you keep doing that?</summary><p>No. One good week is luck-heavy. Breaking stop rules is how the big losses from Survive first happen.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Put a 20-minute weekly review in your calendar for the same time each week. Use the five questions, even if you only have paper trades.

**Key idea:** Once a week, read your journal, split followed-the-plan from broke-the-plan, and change one thing at most.
