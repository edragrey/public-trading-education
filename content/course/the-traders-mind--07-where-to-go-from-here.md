You've reached the end of the course. That's a real achievement: most people who start learning to trade never get past the exciting parts. This last lesson pulls everything together and suggests what to do next.

## The whole course on one page

**Survive first**
- Being right isn't the same as making money. Losses hurt more than gains help.
- Risk a small, fixed percentage per trade. Size the trade from your stop.
- Win rate alone means little. Expectancy, costs and safety matter.
- Leverage doesn't set your risk; size and stop do. Your stop must come before liquidation.

**Read the market**
- A price is the last deal between a buyer and a seller.
- Candles, timeframes, trends and support describe the past, not the future.
- Check volume, liquidity, market cap and supply before you trust a chart.
- Stories and news move markets, often faster than anyone can explain.

**Build a system**
- Write your rules on one page. Six gates, all yes, or no trade.
- Journal every trade. Review weekly. Change one thing at a time, and only with evidence.
- Test cheaply before you risk real money.

**The trader's mind**
- FOMO, revenge trading and overconfidence break rules. Name them.
- Doing nothing is a decision. Daily limits and routines protect you from yourself.

## An honest word

Trading is hard. Most people who try it lose money, especially in the first years. Learning takes time, screen hours and patience, and there will be stretches where nothing seems to work. None of that means you can't learn it, but it does mean that **staying small while you learn** is the most important rule in this whole course.

Also: you don't have to trade at all. Understanding how markets work is valuable on its own, for saving, investing for the long term, or simply not falling for hype and scams. That's a perfectly good place to stop.

## Sensible next steps

1. **Finish your one-page plan** and pre-trade checklist, if you haven't.
2. **Paper trade 30 trades** with the full checklist and journal (Build a system, lesson 6).
3. **Do a weekly review** every week of that period.
4. If, and only if, you followed your plan and the results are reasonable, **go small with real money**, at a fraction of your normal risk.
5. **Keep learning.** Re-read lessons when they become relevant. They'll mean more once you've lived them.

## Try it (no real money)

Write one paragraph to your future self: why you want to learn this, what your rules are, and what you'll do on a bad day. Read it again after your first 30 trades.

**Key idea:** Survive first, read the market honestly, follow a written system, and watch your own mind. Stay small while you learn. Enjoy the ride.
