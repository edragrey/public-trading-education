You now know several tools. The skill is using them in a sensible **order**, so you don't jump straight to a tiny chart and get lost. This lesson puts them together.

## Two words first

**Top-down** means starting with the big picture (a long timeframe) and zooming in step by step.

**Confluence** means several separate clues pointing the same way. One clue is weak; several together are stronger. None is a guarantee.

## The six steps

1. **Higher timeframe: what is the trend?** Weekly or daily. Up, down or sideways?
2. **Mark the key areas:** support, resistance and trendlines.
3. **Where is price now?** Near an area, or in the middle of nowhere?
4. **Check the extras:** moving averages, volume, RSI. Do they agree or disagree?
5. **Zoom in for timing:** a lower timeframe (4-hour or 1-hour) for a precise entry and stop.
6. **Write the plan:** entry, stop (sized with ATR), target, size.

## A worked example

> **Hypothetical coin, made-up numbers.**
> 1. **Weekly:** higher highs and higher lows for months. Uptrend.
> 2. **Daily:** support around $80 (touched three times); a rising trendline close to it; resistance at $120.
> 3. **Now:** price has pulled back from $115 to about $84, near that support and trendline.
> 4. **Extras:** the pullback came on **lower volume**; the 50-day moving average sits near $82; RSI has cooled from overbought to the middle. They broadly agree.
> 5. **4-hour:** price made a higher low and bounced on a bullish candle at $85.
> 6. **Plan:** buy $85. ATR is $4, so stop 2 × ATR below = **$77**. Target near $115, about 3.75R (risk $8, reward $30). Risk 1% of the account.
>
> Several clues agree. It is still only a plan. If the stop is hit, the plan was wrong and the loss is small.

## What you do when clues disagree

If the weekly trend is down but you want a daily buy, or volume rises on a drop, **wait** or skip. A chart with mixed signals is not a failure. It is a chart telling you there is no clear trade.

## Common mistakes

- Starting on a 1-minute chart.
- Counting indicators instead of understanding them.
- Looking only for clues that agree with what you want.
- Skipping the plan, so there is no stop and no size.

## Try it (no real money)

Pick a coin. Walk the six steps on paper, from weekly to 4-hour, and write the plan. Don't trade it; follow it for a week.

**Key idea:** read a chart in order, big picture first, then areas, then extras, then timing, and finish with a written plan.
