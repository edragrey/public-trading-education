The last lesson used the word "trend". Here is the exact way traders decide whether one is there.

## Swing highs and swing lows

Prices don't move in a straight line. They move up, pause, pull back, and move again. The turning points are:

- **Swing high:** a peak, with lower prices on both sides of it.
- **Swing low:** a trough, with higher prices on both sides of it.

## How they define a trend

- **Uptrend:** a series of **higher highs and higher lows**. Each peak and each dip ends above the previous one.
- **Downtrend:** a series of **lower highs and lower lows**.
- **Range (sideways):** highs and lows stay in roughly the same band.

> **Hypothetical example.** A coin makes swing lows at $50, $54, $58 and swing highs at $60, $65, $70. Each is higher than the last: that's an uptrend. If it then falls to $55, below the last low of $58, the pattern of higher lows has **broken**.

## A break in structure

A **break of structure** is when the pattern fails. In an uptrend, the first lower low is a warning that buyers may be losing control. It's not proof of a new downtrend. It simply means the old description no longer fits.

This is a useful way to use structure: not to predict, but to know **when your idea is wrong**. If you bought because of higher lows, a break below the last higher low is a natural place to admit you were wrong.

## Structure depends on timeframe

The same chart can show an uptrend on the daily chart and a downtrend on the 15-minute chart. Always say which timeframe you mean. A common habit is to read the bigger timeframe first, then look closer.

## Common mistakes

- Drawing the swing points after the fact, when the answer is obvious. Practise on a chart you scroll forward one candle at a time.
- Marking every tiny wiggle as a swing. Pick a timeframe and ignore noise below it.
- Treating a break as a certainty.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. What makes an uptrend?</summary><p>Higher highs and higher lows.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. Lows at $20, $18, $15. Highs at $25, $22, $19. Trend?</summary><p>Downtrend: lower highs and lower lows.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. What does a break of structure tell you?</summary><p>The pattern that defined the trend has failed. It's a warning, not a forecast.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. Can one chart be in an uptrend and a downtrend at once?</summary><p>Yes, on different timeframes.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. Why mark swings on a chart you reveal one candle at a time?</summary><p>Marking them with hindsight makes everything look obvious. Live, it isn't.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Open a daily chart. Mark the last six swing highs and lows. Decide the trend. Then reveal the next ten candles and note whether the structure held or broke.

**Key idea:** A trend is higher highs and higher lows (or the reverse). Structure helps you see when your idea is wrong, not what comes next.
