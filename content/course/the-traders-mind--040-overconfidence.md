Most people expect losing streaks to be the hard part. But a run of **wins** causes its own kind of damage, and it's harder to notice because it feels so good.

## One word first

**Overconfidence** is believing you're more skilled, or your next trades are more certain, than the evidence shows.

## What a winning streak does to people

After five or six wins in a row, it's very common to:

- **Raise your size** "because it's working".
- **Loosen the checklist.** Take trades that pass four or five gates, not six.
- **Trade more often**, because everything looks like a setup.
- **Credit skill** for every win, and blame luck for any loss.

## Remember variance

Survive first (the losing streaks lesson) showed that losing streaks are normal even for good strategies. The same is true the other way. **Winning streaks happen by chance too.**

> **Hypothetical example.** A made-up setup that wins 50% of the time. Over 100 trades, a run of 5 or 6 wins in a row is about as likely as a run of 5 or 6 losses. Neither proves anything about skill.

If the streak came from luck, raising your size just before luck evens out is the worst possible timing.

## How big wins become big losses

> **Hypothetical example.** A made-up account.

1. Starts at **$2,000**, risking 1% ($20) per trade.
2. Six wins at 2R each: **+$240**. Account $2,240.
3. Feeling sure, the trader starts risking **5%** (about $112).
4. Three normal losses in a row: about **−$320**.

Account now around **$1,920**, below where it started, after one of the best runs it ever had.

## Staying steady

- **Keep risk per trade fixed** as a percentage. As your account grows, the dollar amount grows on its own. That's enough.
- **Only change size by rule**, written in your plan and decided in advance. For example: "I review my risk % once a quarter, not after a streak."
- **Keep running all six gates**, especially after wins.
- **Look at your journal in R**, not dollars. A streak of +2R wins looks less magical when you see it's normal.

## A good habit after a big win

Take a short break. Same as after a loss. Strong emotions in either direction cloud judgement.

## Common mistakes

- Increasing size after a winning streak.
- Skipping the checklist when things are going well.
- Believing a run of wins proves skill.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. You've won 6 trades in a row. Does that prove your system is excellent?</summary><p>No. Winning streaks happen by chance too. You need many more trades to judge.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. Why is raising your size after a winning streak risky?</summary><p>If the streak was partly luck, normal losses will arrive at the bigger size, and can wipe out the gains quickly.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. How can your trade size grow safely over time?</summary><p>By keeping the same percentage risk. As the account grows, the dollar amount grows automatically.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. A trader wins five in a row and doubles their risk. What's the danger?</summary><p>One normal losing streak now costs much more.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. What good habit follows a big win?</summary><p>Take a break, review the process, and keep risk the same.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Add one line to your trade plan: "I only change my risk % on [a fixed date or after a set number of trades], never right after a streak." Pick your own trigger.

**Key idea:** A winning streak feels like skill, but chance makes them too. Keep your risk fixed and your checklist strict, especially when it's going well.
