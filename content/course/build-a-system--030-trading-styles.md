Before you write rules, decide **what kind of trader you're trying to be**. The style sets your timeframe, how often you trade, how much time it takes, and what costs hurt you most.

## The main styles

| Style | Typical holding time | Charts used | Trades |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Scalping** | Seconds to minutes | 1 to 5 minutes | Dozens a day |
| **Day trading** | Minutes to hours, closed by end of day | 5 minutes to 1 hour | A few a day |
| **Swing trading** | Days to weeks | 4 hours to daily | A few a week |
| **Position trading / investing** | Months to years | Daily to weekly | A few a year |

## What changes with each style

- **Time:** scalping and day trading need you at the screen. Swing trading can fit around a job. Investing needs the least time.
- **Costs:** the more you trade, the more fees and spread add up (see the hidden costs lesson). Fast styles need a very low cost per trade.
- **Stress:** fast styles mean many decisions and quick feedback. Slower styles mean waiting and bigger swings in open profit and loss.
- **Account size:** fast styles often need more capital and discipline to be worth the costs.
- **Sleep:** in crypto, markets run all night. A swing trade with a stop in place can sit while you sleep. A day trade can't.

> **Hypothetical example.** Ana has a full-time job and checks charts twice a day. Day trading would mean missing setups and rushing. Swing trading on the daily chart, with orders and stops placed in advance, fits her day. The best style is the one you can follow consistently.

## Questions to pick yours

1. How many hours a day can I actually watch the market?
2. Can I stay calm making fast decisions, or do I prefer time to think?
3. What do fees take out of each style at my account size?
4. Do I need my trades to survive overnight and weekends?

## Common mistakes

- Choosing a style because it looks exciting online.
- Mixing styles: opening a day trade, then holding it for weeks because it lost.
- Using a tiny timeframe because it gives more action.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. Which style usually holds for days to weeks?</summary><p>Swing trading.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. Why do costs hurt fast styles most?</summary><p>They trade more often, so fees and spread add up quickly.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. Why might someone with a day job pick swing trading?</summary><p>It doesn't need constant screen time; orders and stops can be placed in advance.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. What is "style drift"?</summary><p>Turning a short-term trade into a long hold because it lost, so the plan changes after the fact.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. What's the best style?</summary><p>The one you can follow consistently with your time, nerves and costs.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Write down how many hours a day you could trade, the cost per trade on your platform, and which style fits. Put it at the top of your trade plan.

**Key idea:** Your style sets your timeframe, costs and stress. Choose one that fits your life, then stay in it.
