**Consistency** means following your plan the same way each time, whatever the last result was. It's less exciting than it sounds. That's the point.

## Think in sets of trades

Instead of asking "will this trade win?", ask "if I take my next 20 setups exactly by the rules, how do I expect it to go?"

> **Hypothetical example.** Plan: 40% win rate, 2 to 1 reward, risk 1%. In 20 trades you'd expect about 8 wins and 12 losses: 8 × 2 − 12 × 1 = +4R. But a particular 20 could easily look quite different. The aim is to take all 20 the same way.

## What consistency includes

- Same risk per trade (a fixed percentage).
- Same checklist before entry.
- Same exit rules, including stops.
- Same journal entry afterwards.
- Taking **every** valid setup, and **only** valid setups.

## What breaks it

- Winning streaks that raise confidence and size.
- Losing streaks that make you doubt a good plan.
- Boredom that pushes you into low-quality trades.
- Changing rules mid-trade.

## Boring is good

When your trading feels routine, with the same steps and little drama, that's often a sign you're doing it right. Excitement and dread both suggest emotion is steering.

## Review in batches

Check the plan after a set number of trades (say 20 or 30), not after each one. If the data says it's not working, change it deliberately, one thing at a time, as the earlier lessons on reviews and rule changes describe.

## Common mistakes

- Changing size with recent results.
- Skipping valid setups after a loss.
- Reviewing the plan after every single trade.

## Try it (no real money)

Write your own consistency list: risk, checklist, exits, journal. Tick it off for every paper trade.

**Key idea:** Consistency is doing the same sensible thing, trade after trade, and letting the edge play out.
