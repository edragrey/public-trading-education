Every price you see, whether a coin, a share or a coffee, comes from the same simple tug of war. Once you see it, a lot of market news starts to make sense.

## Two words first

**Demand** is how much of something people want to buy at a given price.

**Supply** is how much of it is available to buy (or being offered for sale) at that price.

## The tug of war

- If **more people want it** and the supply stays the same, buyers compete and the price tends to **rise**.
- If **fewer people want it**, or more of it is for sale, sellers compete and the price tends to **fall**.
- The price where buyers and sellers agree is where trades happen. If conditions change, that price moves.

> **Hypothetical example.** A small coin has 1,000 tokens trading each day at $10. Good news arrives and twice as many people want to buy, but sellers are still only offering the same amount. Buyers have to offer more to get their tokens, so the price moves up until some new sellers are tempted in, or some buyers drop out.

## What shifts demand

- News, stories and excitement (the narratives lesson)
- Cheaper or easier ways to buy
- Confidence and mood (sentiment)
- More money in the system looking for a home (the later money lessons)

## What shifts supply

- New coins being created or released (unlocks, rewards)
- Holders deciding to sell, or to hold
- Rules that limit supply, such as a fixed maximum

Crypto adds a twist: some coins have a **fixed or slowing supply** written into their code, so demand does most of the moving. Others keep creating new coins, so demand must keep growing just to hold the price steady.

## The micro view: one asset's own economics

Microeconomics means looking at one market, one asset, one company. For a coin that means asking: who wants it and why, how many exist, how many are coming, and what stops people from selling? The market cap and unlocks lesson covers the supply side in detail.

## Why "price" is not the same as "value"

A price only tells you what the last buyer and seller agreed to. It does not prove the thing is worth that. When demand is driven by hype rather than use, it can fade fast.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking price moves only because of "news" without asking who is buying and selling.
- Ignoring supply: coins that are about to unlock lots of tokens face more sellers.
- Assuming a rising price means demand will keep rising.

## Go deeper

<details><summary>Elastic and inelastic demand</summary><p>Elastic demand means buyers react strongly to price changes. Inelastic means they do not. Essentials like fuel are inelastic; many speculative coins are very elastic: when the price climbs, buyers vanish, when it falls, they pile in. It is why moves in small markets can be so fast.</p></details>
