Every trade can lose. That sounds obvious, but many traders act as if each one is certain to win, and then feel betrayed when it doesn't.

## What 'accepting the risk' means

It means that **before you enter**, you:

1. Know the most you'll lose if the stop is hit.
2. Have decided that amount is okay to lose, with no regret.
3. Are ready to act on the stop without argument.

If the idea of the stop being hit makes you anxious, the trade is too big, or you don't believe in the plan yet. Reduce size until a stop-out feels like a normal business cost.

## Why this works

Pain comes from a gap between what you expected and what happened. If you expected a possible loss and it's the size you chose, there's very little gap.

> **Hypothetical example.** Account $5,000. You risk 1% ($50). You've accepted that $50 is the price of the trade. The stop is hit: −$50. Annoying, nothing more. Compare the trader who risked 10% ($500) without thinking about it, and now feels sick.

## A pre-trade statement

Try saying (or writing) this before each trade:

> "I'm risking $__ on this trade. I accept I might lose it. If the stop is hit, I'll take it and move on."

If you can't say it honestly, reduce the size.

## Risk isn't only money

It also includes **being wrong**, **missing out**, and **time**. Accepting these is part of the job.

## Common mistakes

- Entering without knowing the exact loss at your stop.
- Sizing for hoped-for profit, not accepted loss.
- Moving the stop to avoid feeling the loss.

## Try it (no real money)

Before your next paper trade, write the pre-trade statement with the exact dollar amount. If you hesitate, halve the size and try again.

**Key idea:** Decide the loss before the trade, then the stop is just a normal business cost.
