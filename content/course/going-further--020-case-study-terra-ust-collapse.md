Second case study. We stick to widely reported facts and say where a figure comes from.

## What happened

**TerraUSD (UST)** was a stablecoin meant to stay worth $1. It wasn't backed by dollars. Instead it relied on an **algorithm**: a linked token, **LUNA**, was supposed to absorb the price swings (users could swap one for the other).

Starting on **9 May 2022**, UST began to lose its $1 peg. As it slipped, more people rushed to swap UST for LUNA, which created more LUNA, which pushed LUNA's price down, which made the peg harder to defend. Within about a week, reports say almost **$45 billion** in market value disappeared (figure from a public encyclopedia summary of press reports; treat it as approximate). The Terra blockchain was temporarily halted.

Before the collapse, the linked **Anchor Protocol** was reported to pay about **19.45%** yield to people who deposited UST. That's far above ordinary savings rates.

## What the design depended on

A stable coin backed by assets can be redeemed for those assets. This one was held up by **confidence and by the price of its sister token**. If confidence broke, there was nothing underneath. That kind of loop can run in reverse very fast, a bank-run style event with no bank.

## Warning signs, in hindsight

These are things a beginner can check **before** buying anything, using the crypto project lesson:

- **A very high yield with no clear source.** Ask: who is paying, and where does the money come from?
- **"Stable" without clear backing.** What can you redeem it for?
- **A design that needs growth to keep working.**
- **Both tokens rely on each other.**
- **Huge concentration:** a lot of the ecosystem depended on one coin.

Not every warning sign means failure, and many projects with these traits survive for a while. But when several are present, size should be zero or tiny.

## Lessons for a trader

1. **Stablecoin doesn't mean safe.** Look at what backs it, who audits it and what happens if everyone leaves.
2. **High yield is a price for risk.** If it looks too good, find where the risk is hidden.
3. **Stops don't work on a coin that falls straight through.** Position sizing does.
4. **Don't put your whole balance in one thing**, however stable it claims to be.

## What this doesn't say

It doesn't say all stablecoins or all of crypto are the same. It shows what happens when a promise isn't backed by assets.

## Try it (no real money)

Pick a stablecoin. Write down what backs it, who holds those assets, how you can redeem it, and what could go wrong. Mark any answer you couldn't verify as "unverified".

**Key idea:** A promise of stability is only as good as what backs it. High yield and unclear backing mean small size or none.
