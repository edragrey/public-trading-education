So far you've looked at one trade at a time. A **portfolio** is all your holdings together, and the way you split them matters more than any single trade.

## Two words first

- **Asset allocation:** how you divide your money between kinds of assets, such as cash, funds, large coins and small bets.
- **Rebalancing:** moving money back to your planned split after prices have shifted it.

## A simple way to split

Many people think in three layers:

1. **Safety:** money you must not lose or will need soon (rent, emergencies). It stays in cash or very safe places, not in trades.
2. **Core:** long-term holdings you intend to keep for years, such as a broad low-cost fund or a few large, established assets.
3. **Trading slice:** a small part you are willing to lose while you learn and practise.

> **Hypothetical example.** You have $10,000 set aside. You keep $4,000 as safety, put $5,500 in your long-term core, and keep $500 as the trading slice. If the trading slice falls 50%, you've lost $250, or 2.5% of the whole. Your learning has a ceiling.

Your split depends on your age, income, goals and how well you sleep when prices drop. There's no perfect one. The point is to **decide it on purpose**.

## Why the slice protects you

A small trading slice gives you room to practise without risking the money you can't lose. It also helps with psychology: when a loss can't hurt your life, you can follow your plan more calmly.

## Rebalancing

Prices move your split without you doing anything. If your core doubles, it becomes a bigger share. Once or twice a year, check your numbers and move money back toward your plan. It forces a calm habit: trimming what grew and topping up what shrank.

> **Hypothetical example.** You planned 70% core and 30% other. After a strong year it's 85% and 15%. You sell a little of the core, or put new money into the other part, to get back to 70/30.

## Common mistakes

- Having no plan for the whole, only for each trade.
- Letting the trading slice quietly grow until it's most of your money.
- Topping up the trading slice after losses to "win it back".
- Checking every hour. A portfolio is for long-term decisions.

## Go deeper

<details><summary>Investing and trading together</summary><p>Many sensible people hold a diversified core for the long term and trade a small slice. The two jobs are different. Mixing them up, such as selling your core in a panic to fund a trade, is where trouble starts.</p></details>

<details><summary>Taxes and rebalancing</summary><p>In some places, selling something that gained can create a tax bill. That can affect how you rebalance. Check your country's rules, or add new money instead of selling.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

On paper, write down a made-up pot of money and split it into safety, core and trading slice. Decide what share is the trading slice and why. Then imagine the slice falls 50% and write how much that costs you in total.

**Key idea:** Decide how your whole pot is split before you decide any trade. A small trading slice lets you learn without risking what you can't lose.
