Welcome to the third module. You can now protect your money (Survive first) and read what a market is doing (Read the market). This module puts the two together into a **system**: a set of rules you write down before you trade.

## One word first

**Trading system** is a written set of rules for what you trade, when you get in, when you get out and how much you risk. Nothing more mysterious than that.

## It doesn't need to be clever

Beginners often think a system means a secret formula. It doesn't. A simple system that you **actually follow** beats a clever one you abandon after three losses. Most of the value comes from three things:

1. **It's written down.** Not "in your head".
2. **It's specific.** Someone else could read it and take the same trades.
3. **You follow it**, including on the days you don't feel like it.

## Why gut feel struggles

Gut feel isn't useless. Experienced traders often have good instincts. But for a beginner, "feel" is mostly mood:

- After two wins, everything looks like a good trade.
- After two losses, nothing does, or you want to win it back quickly.
- When something is all over social media, it feels urgent.

None of those feelings are information about the market. They're information about you. A written system makes the decision **before** the feelings arrive.

## The real reason: you can't improve what you can't measure

> **Hypothetical example.** Two made-up beginners after 30 trades.

- **Sam** traded on feel. Up some weeks, down others, overall down 6%. What went wrong? Sam can't say. Each trade was different, for different reasons.
- **Alex** followed a written system. Also down 6%. But Alex can look back and see: the trades that followed the rules were roughly break even, and the four trades where Alex broke a rule lost most of the money.

Same result. Only Alex knows what to fix. That's the point of a system: it turns your trades into **evidence**.

## What a basic system contains

The rest of this module builds each part. In short:

- **What** you trade (which markets, which timeframe).
- **When you get in** (the situation you're looking for, described plainly).
- **Where you're wrong** (how you place your stop).
- **Where you take profit** (how you set a target, or when you'd exit otherwise).
- **How much you risk** (your fixed % from Survive first).
- **When you don't trade** (big events, daily loss limit, your own state).
