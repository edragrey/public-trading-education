Markets don't just move on numbers. They move on **stories**. "AI will change everything." "This is the next big gaming coin." In crypto especially, money tends to rush into whatever story is hottest, then rush out again. This lesson explains how that usually plays out.

## Two words first

**Narrative** is a story the market is excited about, that ties a group of coins or stocks together. For example "AI tokens", "meme coins", or "electric cars" for stocks.

**Hype cycle** is the rise and fall of attention around a narrative: from almost nobody talking about it, to everybody, and back.

## The usual stages

Not every narrative follows this neatly, but many go through something like it.

1. **Quiet start.** A few people are building or talking about it. Prices barely move. Hardly anyone notices.
2. **Discovery.** Prices of the main coins start rising. More people notice. Articles and posts appear.
3. **Excitement.** It's everywhere. Big daily moves. **Copycat tokens** launch to ride the story, often with little behind them.
4. **Peak attention.** Friends and family ask about it. Your feed is full of it. Volume is huge.
5. **Fade.** New buyers dry up. Early holders sell. Prices fall, often fast. Attention moves on to the next story.

## The uncomfortable truth

By the time a narrative is **all over your feed**, most of its early move has often already happened. The people posting loudest are frequently those who bought earlier. That's not always a scam (see the staying safe lesson in Survive first), but it means the person excited on your screen and you may be on very different points of the same cycle.

Nobody can see in real time exactly which stage it is. Stage 3 can feel like stage 2. Stage 4 can last longer than seems possible. That's why this lesson isn't a timing tool.

## What helps instead

- **Notice the stage, roughly.** Ask: how many people are talking about this? Are copycats appearing? Are people with no interest in markets asking about it?
- **Check the basics from this module.** Is there real volume and liquidity? What's the market cap and how much supply is still to come?
- **Size it like any other trade.** A good story doesn't change your risk per trade. If anything, hyped coins move faster, so stops need more room and the position should be **smaller**.
- **Write down why** you're buying, before you buy. "Because everyone's talking about it" is a feeling, not a reason. The Trader's mind module looks at this as FOMO.

## Common mistakes

- Buying because everyone is talking about it.
- Treating a story as proof of value.
- Holding a hype-driven position with no exit plan.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. A narrative is on every news site and your friends are asking about it. What stage might this be?</summary><p>Possibly late: excitement or peak attention. You can't be sure, but much of the early move has often happened by then.</p></details>

<details><summary>2. Lots of new copycat tokens are launching around one story. What does that suggest?</summary><p>That the story is drawing a lot of attention and money, which often happens in the excitement stage. Copycats frequently have little behind them.</p></details>

<details><summary>3. Should a strong story make you risk more on a trade?</summary><p>No. Your risk per trade stays the same. Hyped coins tend to swing more, so a smaller position is often wiser.</p></details>

<details><summary>4. When is a narrative usually loudest?</summary><p>Often after much of the move has already happened.</p></details>

<details><summary>5. What helps instead of following hype?</summary><p>A written plan with a stop and a size you can afford to lose.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Pick one narrative that's being talked about right now. Write down: which stage you think it's in, two signs that made you think so, and what would make you change your mind. Look back at your note in a month.

**Key idea:** Stories pull money in and push it out again. The loudest moment is rarely the earliest one, so trade the plan, not the hype.
