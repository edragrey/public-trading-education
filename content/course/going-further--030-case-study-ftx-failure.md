Third case study. For this one we rely on a US bankruptcy court record, which is a primary source.

## What happened

**FTX Trading Ltd.**, a large crypto exchange, and its sister trading firm **Alameda Research Ltd.** filed voluntary petitions for protection under **Chapter 11** of the US bankruptcy code on **11 and 14 November 2022**. The US Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware lists these dates in its opinions.

In its description of the failure, the court says the founder's "borrowing" of customer deposits to fund Alameda's investments **left FTX in a precarious position**. Customers could withdraw their funds at any time, but neither FTX Trading nor Alameda had the assets to satisfy customer withdrawal requests.

In plain words: money customers believed was sitting safely on the exchange had been used elsewhere. When many customers asked for their money back, it wasn't all there.

## Why this matters to you

1. **Platform risk is real.** The where you trade lesson said that if you don't hold the keys, you hold a promise. This is what a broken promise looks like.
2. **Customers can be unsecured.** In a bankruptcy, customers may wait a long time and recover only part.
3. **A famous name isn't safety.** Big, well-publicised platforms failed.
4. **Concentration kills.** People with everything on one exchange lost access to everything at once.

## Practical habits

- **Keep only what you're actively trading on an exchange.** Withdraw the rest to a wallet you control, or to a regulated custodian you've researched.
- **Test withdrawals regularly.**
- **Spread across places**, not only across coins.
- **Read how the platform proves its reserves**, and treat claims as claims.
- **Be wary of anything that pays customers more than the market can** or that mixes your deposit with lending.

## What this doesn't say

It doesn't mean every exchange is unsafe, or that self-custody is easy. Self-custody has its own risks. It means your risk is the platform as well as the coin.

## Sources

- US Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, opinions in *In re FTX Trading Ltd.*, Case No. 22-11068 (including the Memorandum Opinion and Orders on Motions to Dismiss in the Alameda adversary proceedings), at deb.uscourts.gov.
