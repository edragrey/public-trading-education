You've learned the pieces. Now put them into **one page you could actually follow**. This is your capstone. Nobody grades it. Its job is to make you decide things calmly now, so you don't have to decide them in a panic later.

Write your answers in a note on your phone or on paper. Use made-up numbers while you practise. Keep the plan short: if it needs three pages, it's too complicated.

## Part 1: Why and how much

1. **My goal.** Why am I doing this? (Learning, extra income, long-term growth, or just curiosity.) Be honest.
2. **My trading slice.** How much money can I lose without it hurting my life? (See *Building a simple portfolio*.)
3. **My risk per trade.** What fixed percent of the slice will I risk on any one trade? (Many beginners pick 1%.)
4. **My daily and weekly stop.** After losing this much, I stop for the day or week.

> **Hypothetical example.** Slice: $500. Risk per trade: 1%, so $5. Daily stop: $15. If I lose $15, I close the screen and go for a walk.

## Part 2: What I trade

5. **My markets.** Which one or two markets will I focus on, and why?
6. **My timeframe.** Which timeframe will I use to decide, and which for timing?
7. **What I will not trade.** For example: new coins I can't explain, anything with high leverage, anything I heard about from a stranger.

## Part 3: The trade itself

8. **My reason to enter.** What has to be true on the chart for me to take a trade? Write it in one or two sentences.
9. **My stop.** Where does my idea fail? Write the rule (for example, 2 × ATR below entry, or under the last swing low).
10. **My position size.** Write the formula: money at risk ÷ distance to stop = size.
11. **My target and my exit plan.** Where will I take some profit? Will I trail a stop? (See *A plan for taking profits*.)
12. **My minimum reward-to-risk.** I won't take trades below, say, 2 to 1.

## Part 4: Me

13. **My warning signs.** What do I do when I'm tired, angry, bored or chasing? (Example: "I close the app and write in my journal.")
14. **After a big win.** What will I do with part of it? (See *Protecting a big win*.)
15. **After a big loss.** What will I do before I trade again? (See *Rebuilding after a big loss*.)
16. **My review.** When will I read my journal? (Example: Sunday evening, 20 minutes.)

## Check your plan

<details><summary>Could someone else follow it without asking me questions?</summary><p>If not, make the rules clearer. Vague rules like "buy when it looks strong" can't be tested or reviewed.</p></details>

<details><summary>Does it say what I do when I'm wrong?</summary><p>If the stop and the daily limit aren't written down, the plan has a hole.</p></details>

<details><summary>Is any rule there only to feel good?</summary><p>Rules should protect you or give you an edge. Delete the rest.</p></details>

<details><summary>Can I afford to follow it for 50 trades in a row?</summary><p>If a normal losing streak would break you, the size is too big.</p></details>

<details><summary>Have I tested it with no real money first?</summary><p>Paper trade it for a few weeks and keep the journal before using real money.</p></details>

## Try it (no real money)

Write the full plan using made-up numbers. Then paper trade it for at least 20 trades, writing a journal line for each. After 20, read it back and change **one** rule at a time.

**Key idea:** A written plan turns hope into rules. Decide while you're calm, test it with no real money, and change one thing at a time.
