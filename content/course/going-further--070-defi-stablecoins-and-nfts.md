Crypto has its own vocabulary. Here are three common words, explained plainly, with the risks first.

## Stablecoins

A **stablecoin** is a crypto token meant to hold a steady value, usually $1.

- **Fiat-backed:** the issuer says it holds dollars or similar assets as backing. Your safety depends on whether that's true and whether you can redeem.
- **Crypto-backed:** backed by other crypto, often more than the coin's value, to cushion falls.
- **Algorithmic:** held steady by code and incentives rather than assets. The Terra case study shows how this can fail.

Risks: the backing may not be there, the issuer can freeze or fail, the peg can break, and platforms that pay high yield on them take risks you may not see.

Check: who issues it, what backs it, whether there are independent reports, and whether you can redeem it.

## DeFi (decentralised finance)

**DeFi** means financial services run by code on a blockchain instead of a company: trading, lending and borrowing, with no sign-up.

- **Decentralised exchange (DEX):** trade straight from your wallet.
- **Lending protocols:** deposit coins to earn interest, or borrow against collateral.
- **Liquidity pools:** people deposit two coins so others can trade between them, and earn fees. They can lose out if prices move (called **impermanent loss**).

Risks:

- **Smart contract bugs.** Code can have flaws, and hackers exploit them.
- **Scam tokens** that you can buy but not sell.
- **Approvals:** letting a site spend your tokens can be abused if you approve the wrong one.
- **Fake sites and links.**
- **No customer support, and no way to reverse a mistake.**
- **High yields** that are paid from new deposits or from risk you can't see.

Habits: use a separate wallet with a small amount, read what you sign, revoke approvals you no longer need, and start with amounts you'd be fine losing.

## NFTs

An **NFT** (non-fungible token) is a unique token on a blockchain, often tied to an image or other digital item. Owning one means holding a record of ownership of that token. It doesn't always give you legal rights to the underlying work.

Risks:

- **Thin markets.** There may be few buyers, so you can't sell when you want.
- **Fakes and copies.**
- **Prices driven by hype** and attention, which can vanish.
- **Phishing** through fake mint pages and airdrops.
- **Rules of ownership** depend on terms that vary a lot.

For most beginners, NFTs are collectibles with a speculative price, not something to size like a normal trade.

## A safety checklist for all three

1. Can I explain in one sentence what it is and where the return comes from?
2. What happens if the company or code fails?
3. Can I sell, withdraw or redeem easily, and have I tested it with a small amount?
4. Is the website address one I typed or verified myself?
5. Is this a size I could lose entirely without harm?

## Common mistakes

- Chasing a yield without asking where it comes from.
- Approving anything a website asks.
- Treating "decentralised" as meaning "safe".
- Using the same wallet for everything.

## Try it (no real money)

Choose one stablecoin, one DeFi protocol or one NFT collection. Answer the five safety checklist questions in writing. Mark anything you couldn't verify as "unverified".

**Key idea:** Stablecoins, DeFi and NFTs each add their own risks. Understand where the return comes from and who can fail before you put money in.
