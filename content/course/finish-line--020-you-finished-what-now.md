If you've reached this page, you've worked through a lot. Take a moment to notice that.

> **Not accredited.** This course is free education. It is **not** a licence, a qualification or an accredited certificate, and it doesn't make you a financial adviser. Nothing here is financial advice.

## What you can now do

- **Explain** what a market, a price, an order and a candle are.
- **Do the maths** for position size, risk, reward-to-risk, break-even win rate and compounding.
- **Protect yourself** with stops, small size, diversification and a plan for leverage.
- **Read a chart** top-down: trend, structure, volume, volatility and a few indicators, with their limits.
- **Understand why prices move**: supply and demand, liquidity, leverage, cycles, central banks, inflation and global events.
- **Build a written system** with rules for entry, exit, taking profits and reviewing your trades.
- **Manage your mind**: fear, greed, overconfidence, revenge trading, and how to rebuild after a big loss.
- **Spot trouble**: scams, hype, and who might be on the other side of your trade.

## What this course is not

- It doesn't predict markets. Nobody can.
- It doesn't promise you'll make money. Most people who trade lose.
- It doesn't replace a qualified adviser for tax, legal or personal money decisions.

## Your next ninety days

1. **Weeks 1 to 4:** paper trade your written plan. Keep the journal. No real money.
2. **Weeks 5 to 8:** review the journal every week. Change one rule at a time.
3. **Weeks 9 to 12:** if, and only if, you've followed the plan on paper and still want to continue, consider a very small real amount from your trading slice. Keep the same rules.

At any point, stopping is a good decision too. Learning how markets work and how you behave under pressure is valuable even if you never trade.

## Keep learning

- Re-read the module cheat sheets once a month.
- Take the final exam again in a few weeks and see what stuck.
- Come back to the lessons on the mind whenever the market gets exciting or painful.

## Common mistakes after finishing

- Going straight to large real money because you "know the theory now".
- Dropping the written plan as soon as a trade goes badly.
- Stopping the journal because it feels like extra work.
