To trade, you need a place that holds your money or assets and sends your orders to the market. There are three common kinds.

## Exchange

An **exchange** is the marketplace itself, with the order book from the last lessons. Stock exchanges and crypto exchanges both work this way.

- In crypto you can often sign up to an exchange directly and trade there.
- Stock exchanges usually don't take retail customers directly. You reach them through a broker.

## Broker

A **broker** is a company that sends your orders to an exchange for you. Stock trading apps are brokers. Many crypto exchanges also act like brokers: you deposit money and they handle the rest.

## Wallet

A **wallet** is where you hold crypto yourself. There are two kinds:

- **Custodial:** a company holds the coins for you, like a bank. Easy, but you trust the company.
- **Self-custody:** you hold the **private key** (the secret that controls the coins). Nobody can freeze your coins, but if you lose the key, they are gone for good. Nobody can reset it.

> **A rule worth remembering.** If you don't hold the keys, you hold a promise from the company that it will give you your coins back. Companies have failed before.

## Centralized vs decentralized exchanges

- **Centralized (CEX):** a company runs it. You make an account, and the company holds your funds while you trade.
- **Decentralized (DEX):** trading happens through code on a blockchain, directly from your own wallet. No sign-up, but you carry more responsibility: wrong address, fake token or a bad approval can cost you everything.

## What to check before you trust a platform

1. **Is it regulated** in your country, and by whom? Look it up on the regulator's own site, not on the platform's.
2. **How long has it operated**, and has it ever lost customer funds?
3. **What are the fees?** (Trading fees, withdrawal fees and the spread all count.)
4. **Does it offer two-factor authentication** (a second code to log in)? Turn it on.
5. **Can you withdraw easily?** Test with a small amount first.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving large amounts on a platform you've barely researched.
- Clicking links from messages or ads instead of typing the address yourself.
- Writing a recovery phrase in a photo, a chat or a cloud note.
- Not testing a small withdrawal before depositing a lot.

## Try it (no real money)

Pick one platform you might use. Find its regulator or licence, its fee page, and its security settings. Write the three facts down. If you can't find them in ten minutes, that's a useful warning in itself.

**Key idea:** Choose where you trade as carefully as what you trade. Check regulation, fees and security, and never put more somewhere than you can afford to lose.
