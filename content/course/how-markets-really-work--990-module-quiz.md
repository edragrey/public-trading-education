## 1. Demand rises and supply stays the same. What tends to happen to price?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="a">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">It rises</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">It falls</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">Nothing</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>Buyers compete for the same amount, so price tends to rise.</p>
</div>

## 2. Why does a large market buy move the price?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="a">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">It eats through sellers at rising prices</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">Exchanges raise prices on big orders</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">It never does</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>It works through the order book, paying more for each next layer.</p>
</div>

## 3. What is slippage?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="b">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">A trading fee</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">The gap between expected and actual price</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">A type of order</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>Slippage is the difference between the price you expected and the price you got.</p>
</div>

## 4. What does a market maker mainly earn from?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="b">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">Betting on direction</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">The spread</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">Mining rewards</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>They post buy and sell prices and earn the gap between them.</p>
</div>

## 5. What is a liquidation cascade?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="a">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">Forced sells pushing price down and liquidating more positions</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">A slow, orderly decline</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">A type of fee</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>Forced selling lowers price, which triggers more forced selling.</p>
</div>

## 6. A strongly positive funding rate suggests:

<div class="quiz" data-answer="a">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">Many traders are long and the trade may be crowded</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">Prices will certainly fall</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">Exchanges are closed</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>It shows crowding, not a certain reversal.</p>
</div>

## 7. Which flow figures are usually measured rather than guessed?

</div>

## 8. Which phase of the cycle has the highest excitement?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="b">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">Accumulation</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">Distribution, near the highs</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">Markdown</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>Excitement peaks near the highs as early buyers sell to late ones.</p>
</div>

## 9. Why can an already expected rate cut do little?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="a">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">It was already priced in</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">Central banks ignore markets</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">Rates do not matter</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>Markets move on surprises against expectations.</p>
</div>

## 10. What is risk-off?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="a">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">Investors move from risky assets to safer ones</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">Investors buy everything</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">Markets close</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>In risk-off, people sell stocks and crypto and move to cash, bonds or the dollar.</p>
</div>

## 11. Why can the first market reaction to a global shock be misleading?

<div class="quiz" data-answer="a">
<button type="button" data-opt="a">Fear and thin liquidity often cause overshoots</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="b">The first reaction is always right</button>
<button type="button" data-opt="c">Markets ignore shocks</button>
<p class="quiz-why" hidden>Early moves often overshoot and then settle as the real effect becomes clearer.</p>
</div>
