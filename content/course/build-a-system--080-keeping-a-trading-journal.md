A system tells you what to do. A journal tells you what you **actually did**. Without one, your memory will quietly rewrite your trades: the wins will feel like skill and the losses like bad luck. This lesson gives you a journal short enough to keep.

## One word first

**Trading journal** is a record of every trade: the plan, what happened, and how you felt. On paper, in a spreadsheet or in an app, it doesn't matter. What matters is that it's complete and honest.

## Two minutes per trade

Big journals get abandoned. Start with this:

```
Date: ______  Market: ______
Plan version: ___

BEFORE
Setup: _______________________
Entry: ______  Stop: ______
Target: ______
Size: ______  Risk: $______
All six gates yes?   Y / N
How I feel (1 word): ________

AFTER
Exit: ______  Result: $______
Result in R: ______
  (result ÷ risk)
Followed my plan?    Y / N
If no, what I broke: _________
One thing I noticed: _________
```
## Two fields that matter most

**Result in R.** "R" means one unit of your planned risk. If you risked $20 and made $40, that's **+2R**. If you lost $20, that's **−1R**. Measuring in R lets you compare trades of different sizes, and it shows straight away if a loss was bigger than planned (−1.6R means slippage, fees, or a moved stop).

**Did I follow my plan?** This one question splits your trades into two groups: the system's results and your mistakes. The expectancy lesson in Survive first and the first lesson of this module both depend on it.

## Fill in "before" before

Write the before part **before** you enter. It's tempting to fill it all in afterwards, but by then you know the result, and the result changes how you remember your reasons.

## Keeping the habit

- **Journal every trade**, including small ones and paper trades. Gaps make the data useless.
- **Same place, same time.** Right after you enter, and right after you exit.
- **Keep it boring.** No essays. If one line covers it, use one line.
- **Note the skipped trades** too, with the gate they failed. They're part of the evidence.

## Common mistakes

- Writing the journal only after you know the result.
- Skipping losing trades.
- Writing long entries you'll never reread.

## Try it (no real money)

Copy the template into a note or spreadsheet. Use it for your next three paper trades, including any you skip.

**Key idea:** Your memory rewrites your trades. A short, honest journal, filled in before and after, is the only record you can trust.
